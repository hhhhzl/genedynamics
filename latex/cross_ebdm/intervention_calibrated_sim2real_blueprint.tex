\begin{abstract}
Different robots may share task semantics while the same skill distribution produces different contact, motion, and failure distributions under different execution mechanisms. We study transfer of a source task-conditioned behavior distribution into behavior executable by a target robot. Starting from an existing source prior, limited interventions calibrate task-relevant execution responses, followed by learning source-conditioned deterministic residual transport. Training always retains the original task loss. Source-response anchoring applies only to samples near matched task-only performance, with sufficiently close current source/target states, low ensemble disagreement, and permitted execution phases. Task-only fallback on independent model validation prevents obvious proxy degradation. This is conditional selective anchoring: soft task bands, public projection, and fallback do not guarantee real task performance or safety. Theory distinguishes response correspondence, model proxies, and actual task effects, and defines attribution boundaries between the public executor and source anchoring. Experiments primarily compare full-system task performance, data requirements, and execution cost against established external methods, using matched task-only and aligned ablations to explain mechanisms and applicability. The first three tasks cover carrying objects into constrained workcells, strict guided-contact alignment, and keyed-connector insertion with recovery. The fourth retains insertion semantics while testing executability under mobile-body/support coupling. Cross-DOF and hardware evidence remain unfinished; superiority on every task, request, or metric is not presumed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Different arms can perform the same alignment or insertion task despite differing kinematics, control delays, compliance, and contact responses. A source policy describes not merely one trajectory but a conditional behavior distribution over initial states, disturbances, and action choices. Deployment on another body or real system can change motion, contact, and failure probabilities. Cross-embodiment and sim-to-real transfer can therefore be viewed jointly as task-relevant distribution transfer constrained by target execution mechanisms.

Shared state/action representations support skill reuse across bodies\cite{lsa,lad,xskill}; system identification and active exploration improve deployment through limited real experiments\cite{asid,spi,optimalexploration}; distribution-alignment methods already combine joint representation alignment and OT/UOT for sim-and-real learning\cite{otreal}. Human2Sim2Robot further demonstrates object-level task semantics across the embodiment gap\cite{human2sim}. These form the methodological foundations here.

Similarity of representations or command distributions does not automatically imply similarity of real interaction responses. Accurate identified parameters also do not determine how source multimodal behavior should change on target. We focus on constructing, from limited real interventions, a behavior distribution \emph{realized by target mechanisms while preserving useful task structure}. Successful target-trajectory distributions are generally not fully observed; they are optimization objects jointly determined by task goals, source priors, and calibrated target mechanisms.

Two arms can have similar free-space trajectories yet yield recovery versus jamming after lateral offsets at an opening. Multiple source detours or contact-adjustment directions may not all fit the target workspace. Transfer must alter realization and probability structure while permitting necessary task-relevant deviations; unconditional pursuit of distributional similarity is inappropriate.

Sample short skills from an existing source prior, calibrate target responses with a few real probes, define relevant discrepancies through task-phase-dependent geometry, and learn source-conditioned transport. Deterministic maps can already retain multimodality from a stochastic source prior. Add transport noise only to express additional conditional uncertainty, and validate its benefit separately. Train in a calibrated model; at deployment, generate target skills directly from the current actual state and execute closed loop. Action decoding uses existing robot interfaces.

\subsection{Three Research Questions}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{RQ\arabic*.}]
\item \textbf{System effectiveness.} What advantages and tradeoffs does the full transfer method offer against established external methods in constrained task performance, target-data demand, and online execution cost?
\item \textbf{Calibration and selective transport.} Under which mechanisms and budgets do limited calibration, source-response correspondence, and selective constraints help? How do matched task-only and aligned ablations distinguish these effects from public execution, continued training, and model selection?
\item \textbf{Embodiment extension.} Can shared task semantics and skill interfaces execute across arm kinematics and mobile-support morphologies, and which constraints determine applicability?
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Three Candidate Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{C\arabic*.}]
\item \textbf{Modeling executable distribution transfer.} Separate task-conditioned source priors from target closed-loop behavior distributions, define realizable transport through target execution mechanisms, and treat the target marginal as a behavior law to construct.
\item \textbf{Intervention-calibrated selective realization-aware transport.} Combine task-modulated response geometry, low-dimensional mechanism calibration, execution constraints, and source-conditioned mapping to learn target behavior distributions. Regulate response constraints by model support/task compatibility and test benefits, bias, and applicability through ablations. Action decoding is shared execution infrastructure; existing generative models can supply priors.
\item \textbf{Empirical task, resource, and morphology validation.} Compare full systems against established external methods, use internal ablations to explain calibration/response-transfer mechanisms, and test shared skill semantics across bodies. Cross-task benefits may have explicit scope and tradeoffs; hardware evidence is established separately.
\end{enumerate}
Methods and experiments establish the main contributions. Task-relevant exploration, OT/UOT, flow matching, Lagrange updates, and action mapping have precedents and are not individually claimed as new concepts.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\subsection{Cross-Embodiment Representations and Behavior Transfer}
Latent Space Alignment combines encoding/decoding, cycle consistency, and latent dynamics for cross-robot skill alignment\cite{lsa}. Latent Action Diffusion uses semantically aligned latent actions for diffusion policies across end effectors\cite{lad}; XSkill studies cross-embodiment skill discovery\cite{xskill}. Human2Sim2Robot derives shared task rewards from object trajectories\cite{human2sim}. We adopt shared task semantics and study closed-loop realization of behavior distributions under differing mechanisms once representations are given.

\subsection{Sim-to-Real Adaptation and Active System Identification}
Dynamics randomization, GAT, and RMA respectively provide randomization, action grounding, and history adaptation\cite{dr,gat,rma}. ASID and SPI-Active actively collect information and identify target mechanisms\cite{asid,spi}; optimal exploration involving Guanya Shi already studies task-relevant experimental design\cite{optimalexploration}. We use mechanism information from limited experiments to construct target behavior laws, without claiming task-aware exploration itself as the main novelty.

Recent ASAP learns a delta-action model from target data and fine-tunes the source policy in an aligned simulator\cite{asap}. FADA separates motion planning from inverse dynamics, freezing the former and adapting the latter with limited target logs\cite{fada}. Both natively study humanoid dynamics adaptation; arm-contact ports must retain complete training chains and state their adaptation scope. Play2Perfect learns precise contact through multi-object play pretraining and assembly fine-tuning\cite{play2perfect}; ACP learns spatially/temporally variable compliance from force demonstrations\cite{acp}. These are important competing routes for direct task-policy/contact-control learning after calibration. Simple action scaling cannot substitute for complete comparisons.

\subsection{Distribution Alignment and Transport for Robot Learning}
OT-sim2real combines joint representation-distribution alignment, UOT, and temporally correlated sampling for sim-and-real co-training, primarily using limited target demonstrations\cite{otreal}. DARA and H2O+ study source/target learning under dynamics gaps\cite{dara,h2oplus}. Combining path costs/constraints with flow matching also has precedents\cite{densityflow}. Our focus is joint modeling of intervention-calibrated contact response, target executability, and source behavior structure; joint distribution alignment, partial transport, and constrained flows alone are not new contributions.

\section{Preliminary}
\label{sec:prelim}
\subsection{Embodiment-Conditioned Control and Action Decoding}
Environment $e$ has raw observations $o_e$, actions $a_e$, and low-level closed-loop control. Shared state $q=\phi_e(h_e)$ includes relative object/TCP pose, task phase, and contact/force history, augmented by robot capability/configuration summaries where needed. Joint dimensions across bodies are not forcibly mixed.

Shared skill commands $U\in\R^{H\times d}$ specify short-horizon task-space commands, force setpoints, and allowed compliance parameters. Execute through existing action decoder $\mathcal A_e$ and feedback controller $C_e$:
\begin{equation}
 a_{t+j}=C_e\big(h_{t+j};\mathcal A_e(U_j,q,c_e)\big),
 \qquad j=0,\ldots,H-1.
 \label{eq:decode}
\end{equation}
Standardize frames, units, control durations, and permitted action bounds. Actual contact force is a response; a force setpoint is a command. No new decoder architecture or separate decoder contribution is proposed. Theory assumes shared state sufficiently describes macro-step interactions on the declared support.

Mobile bodies use the same task-frame skill $U$; $\mathcal A_e,C_e$ realize end-effector references through arm, base, or leg whole-body control. Target-body state additionally includes base pose/velocity, support contacts, joint margins, and controller history. TCP pose alone cannot establish cross-body state sufficiency. Shared response correspondence compares task progress, object/TCP state, and task contact. Body-specific tipping prevention, foot/wheel slip, and execution limits enter target feasibility/risk; mobile bodies need not reproduce fixed-arm base motion. UMI-on-Legs already connects manipulation policies and WBC through task-frame end-effector trajectories and transfers fixed-arm policies to quadrupeds. We must validate calibration/transport increments beyond this interface, rather than claim the interface itself\cite{umilegs}.

\subsection{Conditional Behavior Distributions and Transport}
The source prior is an existing policy or generative model:
\begin{equation}
 U_s\sim p_s(U\mid q,g).
\end{equation}
Let $c_t$ denote available target calibration context. Learn transport kernel
$T_\varphi(dU_t\mid U_s,q,g,c_t)$, where $U_t$ is the actual request after fixed public
execution interface $\eta$, not an unprojected network candidate. For given $q,g,c_t$,
it induces target command distribution
\begin{equation}
 p_{t,\varphi}(dU_t\mid q,g,c_t)
 =\int T_\varphi(dU_t\mid U_s,q,g,c_t)\,p_s(dU_s\mid q,g).
 \label{eq:kerneltransport}
\end{equation}
The deterministic special case, at the same $q,g,c_t$, is
$p_{t,\varphi}(\cdot\mid q,g,c_t)
=\Phi_{\varphi,\eta}(\cdot;q,g,c_t)\#p_s(\cdot\mid q,g)$.
Here $\eta$ fixes execution and candidate admission; subsequent distribution notation
omits this fixed parameter. Marginal commands across target histories/calibration
contexts also integrate over the policy's actual visitation distribution, rather than
simply pushing forward an unconditional source prior. Additional latent sampling must
enter the kernel's sampling law; a fixed latent adds no sampling multimodality under
the same condition. Full map $\Phi_{\varphi,\eta}$ includes transformation from learned
candidates to actual requests. The realized target behavior law is
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb P_{t,\varphi}^{g}
 =\operatorname{Rollout}_{K_t,\pi_{t,\varphi}}\#\mu_0.
 \label{eq:pathlaw}
\end{equation}
$p_{t,\varphi}$ and $\mathbb P_{t,\varphi}^{g}$ are distinct: the former is at command
level; target feedback, dynamics, and disturbances realize the latter. Compare source
and target responses under shared initial/task conditions; differing initial-state
marginals are not mechanism differences.

Reweighting changes mass on existing support; transport can also move target command support. For an analytic example, let $y_s=u_s$ and $y_t=\kappa u_t$, with source support $[-1.1,-0.9]\cup[0.9,1.1]$. At $\kappa=0.5$, preserving corresponding outputs requires $u_t=2u_s$. Reweighting within the original compact support cannot produce that command distribution. If target bounds forbid $2u_s$, irreducible task-response deviation must be accepted.

\subsection{Problem Formulation}
Inputs are a source behavior prior, shared task goals, available target robot model/control interface, and real calibration interaction with total budget $B$. Output is a target conditional behavior distribution satisfying execution constraints. A successful target-trajectory law is not given in advance.

For current $q,g$, formulate short-horizon transfer using conditional trajectory laws of fixed length $H$:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \min_{Q,\Gamma}\quad&
 \E_{\Gamma}\big[C_g(\tau_t)+\lambda c_g(\tau_s,\tau_t)\big],\\
 \text{s.t.}\quad&\Gamma_s=\mathbb P_s^{g,H}(\cdot\mid q),\quad \Gamma_t=Q,
 \quad Q\in\mathfrak P_t^H(q),\\
 &\text{target execution and risk constraints},
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:ideal}
\end{equation}
$\mathfrak P_t^H(q)$ contains distributions induced by target mechanisms and permitted short-horizon closed-loop execution. Implementation parameterizes a subset with $T_\varphi$, constructing couplings from source conditions and random seeds. Deployment reconditions at every actual target state, inducing the full-task law in Eq.~\eqref{eq:pathlaw}. Concatenated source references generally do not preserve the full-task marginal of independent source rollouts. We do not claim a global OT optimum over all couplings.

Controlled protocol A uses only declared calibration experiments, without target-task demonstrations or target RL fine-tuning. Supplementary B-log permits charged complete self-collected trajectories without reward/expert demonstrations; B-online permits charged task interaction and reward; C-supervised lists target demonstrations/external supervision separately; D-oracle is ground-truth diagnosis only. Methods such as OT-sim2real and LAD requiring target demonstrations are compared under applicable supervision protocols. All calibration, action-interface fitting, and validation count toward $B$; final evaluation does not train the method.

\section{Method: Intervention-Calibrated Distribution Transport}
\label{sec:method}
\textbf{Core workflow.} Fix the source prior and define task-response representations; calibrate target mechanisms with limited feasible interventions; learn transport in the calibrated model; sample/execute target skills from actual current state at deployment. Novelty must arise from these four steps jointly and their empirical effects.

\subsection{Task-Modulated Response Representation}
\label{sec:geometry}
\textbf{Represented quantities.} Each skill execution yields $Y=\Psi_g(\tau)$: task progress, object/TCP geometry, contact-force statistics/time series, tracking errors, contact modes, and failure events. Future trajectories in source records are used only offline; online transport receives currently available history only.

\textbf{Task geometry.} Use a dimensionally normalized distance
\begin{equation}
 d_g^2(y,y';q)=(y-y')^\top G_g(q)(y-y'),\qquad
 G_g(q)=\epsilon I+\sum_j\omega_j(q,g)b_jb_j^\top,\quad\epsilon>0.
 \label{eq:metric}
\end{equation}
Weights $\omega_j$ depend on phase, contact state, goal region, and active constraints.
Constrained approach emphasizes whole-arm/object clearance and pose progress; fixture
alignment emphasizes pressure, sliding, and rotation; insertion emphasizes lateral
contact, advance, and jam/recovery branches. Mode-probability differences enter separately
through one-hot/event costs, so average force cannot hide failure-probability differences.
$\epsilon I$ prevents ignoring directions affecting future decisions. If only selected
features remain, theory and claims are restricted to that quotient space.

\textbf{Trajectory cost.} For trajectories at nearby task phases, define
\begin{equation}
 c_g(\tau_s,\tau_t)=
 \sum_{k=0}^{H}d_g^2(\Psi_{g,k}(\tau_s),\Psi_{g,k}(\tau_t);q_k^s)
 +\lambda_m c_{\rm mode}(\tau_s,\tau_t).
 \label{eq:semantic}
\end{equation}
The first version fixes skill duration. Mechanisms with substantially different speeds
may use bounded phase alignment while retaining a separate completion-time cost.
Arbitrary time warping must not erase real delay or force overshoot. Shared task cost
$C_g$ reflects progress, contact maintenance, failure, and completion. Source-semantic
and target-task costs coexist, permitting source-behavior changes needed for task success.

\textbf{Multimodal structure.} Preserve multiple valid modes $m$ in the source prior, such as left/right adjustments or different detours. Initially label modes with interpretable geometric rules and measure coverage within target-executable modes. Labels discourage averaging distinct behaviors into invalid commands; they do not imply every source mode should survive on target.

\textbf{Implementation and ablation.} Use TCP/object state and wrist F/T, without a large vision model initially. Compare uniform metrics, nominal geometry only, average response, and full mode/disturbance-response representations. This incorporates 2GO's task-modulated geometry insight.

\subsection{Budgeted Intervention and Target-Mechanism Calibration}
\label{sec:calibration}
\textbf{Mechanism model.} Pretrain short-horizon response models in a multi-mechanism simulator:
\begin{equation}
 (q',Y)\sim\widehat K_\psi(\cdot\mid q,U,\delta,c),
 \label{eq:model}
\end{equation}
$c$ is low-dimensional execution context, e.g. command gain, delay, contact compliance,
or friction. Real interaction calibrates $c_t$ and predictive uncertainty:
\begin{equation}
 \hat c_t=\arg\min_c
 \sum_{(q,U,\delta,y)\in\mathcal B_{\rm fit}}
 -\log\widehat K_\psi(y\mid q,U,\delta,c).
 \label{eq:calibrate}
\end{equation}
An existing target simulator can first calibrate parameters, then train a differentiable
short-horizon surrogate. All real target interaction, including reaching contact,
failures, and independent validation, counts toward $B$, reported in control steps
and seconds. Public simulated pretraining and calibrated-twin optimization have separate
compute budgets; controls share available models and compute allowances. Limited real
samples calibrate structured models rather than arbitrary world models from scratch.
Physical context stays within declared physical support; compare discrete delay hypotheses
explicitly. Free latent coordinates cannot still be interpreted as true physical parameters.

\textbf{Intervention semantics.} $\delta$ denotes randomly assignable commands,
initial offsets, or experimental configurations. Define response under the specified
execution interface:
\begin{equation}
 K_e^\delta(dy\mid q,U)
 =\Prob_e(Y\in dy\mid q,\operatorname{do}(U),
 \operatorname{do}(\Delta=\delta);\mathcal A_e,C_e).
 \label{eq:intervention}
\end{equation}
Matched initial states, recorded history, and randomized assignment reduce behavior-selection
bias. Initial probes cover free-space gain/delay, contact establishment, tangential
speeds, lateral offsets, and bounded recovery. Force monitoring uses existing execution
thresholds; limited data do not establish global safety for unknown contact.

\textbf{Budget allocation.} Let probes form a finite candidate set $\Delta_F$, each
satisfying MDOC-style known feasibility constraints. Initially use established
task-dependent design to estimate reductions in relevant response uncertainty:
\begin{equation}
 \min_{\mu}\ \Phi_g(\mu),\qquad
 \E_\mu c(\delta)\le b,\quad\operatorname{supp}\mu\subseteq\Delta_F,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_g(\mu)=
 \begin{cases}
 \tr(B_gF_\mu^\dagger B_g^\top),&\ker F_\mu\subseteq\ker B_g,\\
 +\infty,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:probe}
\end{equation}
This supports the method; task-relevant exploration is not newly introduced here.
An extension could use virtual ensemble measurement updates to select probes maximally
reducing transport-cost uncertainty. Ablate it separately rather than mixing it with
fixed-design Gaussian formulas.

\textbf{Separate calibration and validation.} Fit mechanism/interface parameters on
the fitting set; independently validate task modes, response distributions, and uncertainty.
Revalidate after action-interface changes. All fitting, validation, and failed probes
count toward $B$. Mark uncovered contact branches unknown. Recheck model support when
source priors or transport change visitation, rather than mistaking calibration-fixture
coverage for arbitrary task coverage.

\subsection{Realization-Aware Distribution Transport}
\label{sec:transport}
\textbf{Parameterization and residual reference.} The current network outputs bounded
residuals on public target requests. Let $U_b=B(q,g,\hat c_t,m)$ be the base request
chunk generated by the target task controller from available history, phase, and its
own memory, corresponding to \texttt{base\_proto}. Source queries supply $U_s$ and
history features as network conditions; $U_b$ need not equal $U_s$. In shared normalized
request coordinates, the candidate is
\begin{equation}
 U_t^{\rm cand}=\Phi^{\rm cand}_\varphi(U_s,q,g,\hat c_t,m)
 =\operatorname{Bound}_{q,\hat c_t}
   \big[U_b+\Delta_\varphi(U_s,U_b,q,g,\hat c_t,m)\big].
 \label{eq:residual}
\end{equation}
$m$ is the requested source behavior mode; queried source-history features are omitted
from $\Phi^{\rm cand}_\varphi$ for brevity. $\operatorname{Bound}_{q,\hat c_t}$ applies
known command bounds, local residual limits, and task-owned-channel projection. This
remains at request level. Only after public feedback decoding, recovery, and declared
runtime admission does the candidate become actual request $U_t$. Actual responses
come from $K_t$, training predictions from $\widehat K_\psi$. Losses use effective
requests under the corresponding training rules, not merely preprojection outputs;
training recovery masks do not automatically cover every runtime selector. Randomness
comes from the source prior; the current residual network adds no transport noise.

Task-owned channels limit learnable support. For example, guided-contact transport
may change tangential/orientation commands only; public task control generates normal
contact, stiffness, and desired force. Changing networks does not change this authority.
Study adaptation of those channels through a separately declared action class, matching
action authority/public execution exactly between ours and internal controls such as
matched task-only. External baselines retain native actions/control structures defined
by their methods. Match public observations, physical tasks, safety boundaries, and
resource accounting, and disclose each necessary adaptation/additional recovery mechanism.

\textbf{Feedback and expressive support.} Source realization includes actual contact
phase and pose error; contact, withdrawal, and alignment events advance phases, not
absolute clocks alone. Source priors and transport may condition on available history.
Ours and matched internal ablations share decoder pose feedback. Source stopping does
not imply target equilibrium, so $|U_s|$ must not hard-zero every corresponding target
correction direction. Bounded residuals and actual $SE(3)$ feedback maintain terminal
equilibrium. Project normal-position references during force control to prevent unused
reference accumulation and jumps on release. These shared feedback repairs are not
independent transport contributions. External baselines use declared control structures;
report the role/evaluation scope of any added public feedback or recovery separately.

Conditional flow is a possible future parameterization:
\begin{equation}
 \frac{dX_\ell}{d\ell}=v_\varphi(X_\ell,\ell;U_s,q,g,\hat c_t,m),
\quad X_0\sim\mathcal N(0,I),\quad
U_t^{\rm cand}=\operatorname{Bound}_{q,\hat c_t}(U_b+X_1).
 \label{eq:flow}
\end{equation}
This is a source-conditioned transport kernel; $\ell$ is generation/transport time,
not physical time. It is outside the frozen main method/claims. If added, compare
against the residual map under identical task objectives, data, and budgets. A
parameterization ablation may replace one residual MLP with a short deterministic
denoising chain, retaining source conditioning, task loss, action projection, and
execution authority. If trained end-to-end only by our task objective, without noise
prediction or score matching, it tests iterative parameterization, not learned
probabilistic scores or automatic mode expansion beyond the source prior.

\textbf{Actual response objective.} The current implementation does not impose all
source responses on the target. For a training sample, let $C_i(\varphi)$ be original
task cost in the calibrated model, $C_i^T$ the frozen matched task-only policy's cost
on the same batch, and $R_i(\varphi)\geq0$ source-conditioned response cost.
Define the additive task-band hinge
\begin{equation}
 H_i(\varphi)=\big[C_i(\varphi)-C_i^T-\delta\big]_+ .
 \label{eq:additive-band}
\end{equation}
Task loss $C_i$ remains inside and outside the band. $H_i$ adds a penalty beyond the
matched task-only model-proxy tolerance; it does not replace $C_i$.

Response anchoring acts only through a detached offline-training gate. Let
$d_i^{\rm state}$ be normalized pose/wrench distance between current target and queried
source state, $d_i^{\rm ens}$ response-ensemble disagreement, and $M_i^{\rm exec}$ the
phase-specific mask permitting command changes. Then
\begin{equation}
 G_i=M_i^{\rm exec}
 \ind\{C_i(\varphi)\le C_i^T+\delta\}
 \ind\{d_i^{\rm state}\le q_{.95}^{\rm state}\}
 \ind\{d_i^{\rm ens}\le q_{.95}^{\rm ens}\}.
 \label{eq:selective-gate}
\end{equation}
Both quantile thresholds use source/known-nominal calibration only; $G_i$ receives
no gradient. $d_i^{\rm state}$ does not measure distance between candidate actions
and response-model training actions. $d_i^{\rm ens}$ is not a coverage-calibrated error
bound; action support needs separate checks. $G_i$ controls training loss, not the
deployment candidate selector. Recovery tasks incorporate recovery masks in
$M_i^{\rm exec}$ so response loss compares effective actions actually used by recovery.
The frozen core objective is
\begin{equation}
 J_{\rm gated}(\varphi)=
 \E_i\!\left[C_i(\varphi)+\beta H_i(\varphi)
       +\lambda G_i R_i(\varphi)+\Omega_i(\varphi)\right].
 \label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
$\Omega_i$ contains shared model-risk and command-change penalties, the latter relative
to $U_b$. Core training uses $\delta=0.05$ and $\beta=100$, model-side constants rather
than actual task tolerances/safety margins. The core binary selection compares the
trained candidate checkpoint with matched task-only: if independent held-out model
task cost exceeds task-only by $0.05$, fall back to the task-only checkpoint. This
offline short-horizon model-proxy selection does not replace episode evaluation or
runtime admission. Implementations adding progress loss, per-mode checkpoints, or
multiple-candidate validation must state their training/selection procedures separately;
they are not automatically equivalent to this binary rule. Sealed final target data
never select checkpoints. Passing model selection does not establish task gains from
source anchoring. To isolate it, both ablation branches share warm start, data, sample
stream, optimizer, update/validation budgets, and checkpoint rules. A task-trained
checkpoint may initialize both if subsequent training opportunities match; do not give extra refinement only to the
anchoring branch. With shared public decoder,
completion, and feasibility projection $P$, compare $P\circ\phi_A$ and $P\circ\phi_T$.
This is a component-attribution requirement, not an algorithmic rule demanding per-request
victory over task-only before deployment. External full-system comparisons allow
reasonable independent training procedures with declared costs.

Omitting shared $\Omega$, the six cells are frozen-warm, $C$, $C+R$, $C+H$, $C+H+R$,
and $C+H+G R$. The first four objectives $C,C+R,C+H,C+H+R$ form a strict ungated
$2\times2$; only $C+H+G R$ versus $C+H+R$ isolates selective gating. Historical
band-only/Full objectives replacing $C$ with $H$ are not this implementation.

\textbf{From coupling to distribution.} Eq.~\eqref{eq:objective} optimizes the joint
law of $U_s\sim p_s$ and $U_t\sim T_\varphi(\cdot\mid U_s)$, with target marginal
Eq.~\eqref{eq:kerneltransport}. It changes the entire target behavior distribution
and support rather than reweighting source replay. Compare realized response law
\begin{equation}
 \rho_{t,\varphi}^{\delta}(dy\mid q,g,\hat c_t)
 =\int K_t^\delta(dy\mid q,U_t)\,
 T_\varphi(dU_t\mid U_s,q,g,\hat c_t)\,p_s(dU_s\mid q,g).
 \label{eq:responselaw}
\end{equation}
Within-batch Wasserstein/UOT response alignment can relax overly strict samplewise
coupling, as an extension using existing tools. Start with interpretable coupling
and target task cost, then compare nominal-feature and realized-response UOT.
Unmatched UOT mass does not make actual tasks disappear; the policy remains a normalized
behavior law.

\textbf{Execution support and observable effects.} Let $\eta$ include public projection,
decoder, and candidate admission. If two transports have identical actual-request
kernels after $\eta$ at every reachable history $h$, they induce identical closed-loop
path laws under shared initial states/target dynamics. Different internal residuals
or response losses then cannot change task distributions. Finite residual radii,
saturation projection, and task-owned channels can compress observable treatment
differences. Beyond matched action authority, attribution studies must report effective-
request separation, channel-bound occupancy, and selector admission rates. Network
parameters or unprojected candidates alone cannot establish changed behavior from
source-response anchoring.

\textbf{Hard constraints and contact risk.} Known kinematic, velocity/command-rate,
and workspace limits define feasibility. Eq.~\eqref{eq:additive-band} is a soft penalty;
Eq.~\eqref{eq:selective-gate} is a training mask. Neither is a real-system invariant
set or safety certificate. Model-dependent risk may use ensembles:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal R_j(\varphi)=
 \sup_{M\in\mathcal M_{\mathcal B}}
 \operatorname{CVaR}_{1-\delta_j}
 \left[\max_k h_j(\hat q_k,\hat U_k)\right]\le b_j.
 \label{eq:risk}
\end{equation}
Tighten using independent error estimates where necessary. This is model-conditional
risk, not a hardware safety certificate. Real execution remains within existing
validation scope and monitoring.

\textbf{Optional constrained extension.} Future explicit CVaR constraints may use
standard Lagrange multipliers to coordinate objectives and risk:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L(\varphi,\lambda)
 =J(\varphi)+\sum_j\lambda_j(\mathcal R_j(\varphi)-b_j),
 \qquad\lambda_j\ge0,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
 \varphi\leftarrow\varphi-\eta_\varphi\nabla_\varphi\mathcal L,
 \qquad
 \lambda_j\leftarrow[\lambda_j+\eta_\lambda(\widehat{\mathcal R}_j-b_j)]_+.
 \label{eq:lagrange}
\end{equation}
Public execution and online monitoring enforce hard constraints; objectives encode
soft semantic costs. The frozen comparison uses Eq.~\eqref{eq:objective} and public
monitoring. Lagrange/CVaR extensions absent from the main experiments are not completed modules.

\textbf{Training implementation.} Initially use a differentiable short-horizon surrogate,
fixing $p_s$ and calibrated models while backpropagating into transport. Frozen response
parameters still allow input gradients through candidate requests; source/reference
branches and gates use stop-gradient. Pretrain on public mechanisms, then calibrate
target context; hidden target truth is excluded. If a calibrated physics twin is
available, generate equal-budget data or train residual actors with existing SAC,
retaining source-semantic/risk objectives. With only latent surrogates, changing
optimizers retains the same model-error limitation. Report both routes separately;
black-box contact is not assumed differentiable.

\subsection{Training Algorithm and Amortized Closed-Loop Deployment}
\label{sec:deployment}
\textbf{Complete offline/calibration algorithm.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Train/fix source-conditioned priors using existing policies, retaining multiple source modes and source-dynamics information.
\item Choose $B$ calibration interactions within declared candidates; separate fitting from independent validation, then fit target context and ensemble uncertainty.
\item Check action interfaces and mode support, fix interfaces, and mark insufficiently validated regions.
\item Sample $q,g,m,U_s,\delta$, obtain $U_t$ through $T_\varphi$, and roll out source/calibrated-target models.
\item Compute $C,H,G R,\Omega$ and update transport; record training-gate coverage, source/target state distances, candidate action support, ensemble disagreement, and task proxies.
\item Apply matched task-only $+0.05$ checkpoint fallback on independent model validation. Lock final methods, thresholds, and rejection rules before held-out target evaluation. Test components with training, selection budgets, and public execution matched.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Online candidate generation and execution.} At each control time, read actual
$q$, query a source skill, and generate $U_t^{\rm cand}$. Current request chunks are
$0.2\,\mathrm{s}$; every $20\,\mathrm{ms}$, recompute from fresh feedback and execute
the first request, with closed-loop low-level control. The basic path uses public
decoding/task recovery; unsupported source queries fall back to public task requests.
Offline gate $G_i$ does not recompute training losses here.

\textbf{Runtime admission is a separate policy component.} Online-admission implementations
also generate matched task-only/public reference requests and compare candidates/finite
mixtures after common decoding. Some recovery implementations use frozen response
ensembles to predict task proxies and select requests using progress, phase, and
constraint rules. If none passes, execute the reference. These implementations call
response models online and are neither one-forward-pass nor model-free deployment.
With fixed decoder/candidate set/admission $\eta$, the actual policy is
$\pi_{\varphi,\eta}$ and its behavior law is
$\operatorname{Rollout}_{K_t,\pi_{\varphi,\eta}}\#\mu_0$.
Earlier shorthand $\pi_{t,\varphi}$ assumes fixed $\eta$; changing the selector changes
the policy.

Pure amortized execution can choose $\eta$ containing only public execution rules,
but requires separate evaluation. Online-admission results do not automatically apply
to that simplified path. Parameterization controls and anchoring ablations must fix
the same $\eta$ and count every policy/response call, candidate comparison, and
end-to-end latency. Account separately for offline checkpoint fallback, online
candidate fallback, and public hold/retreat/recovery. Unfinished tasks remain failures;
no fallback may provide supervision available only to our method.

\section{Supporting Analysis}
\label{sec:theory}
This section connects identification, response error, and transport cost conditionally.
The conditions themselves require validation; experimental success cannot establish them retrospectively.

\subsection{Task-Relevant Identifiability}
Within a fixed local contact mode, let
\begin{equation}
 y=A_{\mathcal B}\theta+\xi,\qquad b_g=B_g\theta.
\end{equation}
\begin{proposition}
Task response $b_g$ is identifiable under noiseless calibration if and only if
\begin{equation}
 \ker A_{\mathcal B}\subseteq\ker B_g.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
Calibration must cover mechanism directions affecting transport, not necessarily all
physical parameters. If the null-space condition fails, a pseudoinverse trace cannot
fabricate zero uncertainty. Fixed-design Gaussian analysis yields response confidence
regions; adaptive sampling requires appropriate confidence tools. This standard linear-
identification proposition is not a standalone original contribution.

\subsection{Probe Coverage, Identification and Hybrid Events}
\textbf{Nonidentifiability boundary.} If two mechanisms produce identical observation
laws under every allowed probe but require different task responses or successful
action sets, no probe-only algorithm can guarantee correct adaptation to both.
Parameters active only during contact cannot be identified solely from noncontact
observations. Free-space identification can extrapolate to contact only with correct
structure and sufficient excitation of relevant parameters. Lack of contact does
not itself render every gain/delay parameter unidentifiable.

\textbf{Sufficient conditions for finite structured families.} For finite candidate
mechanisms $\Theta_F$, let $z(\theta),\hat z(\theta)$ be actual/model signatures of
a fixed probe set, with the true mechanism among the candidates. Suppose observation
noise norm is at most $\eta$ and probe-model bias at most $\epsilon_p$ for every
candidate. If
\begin{equation}
 \min_{\theta\ne\theta'}\|\hat z(\theta)-\hat z(\theta')\|>2(\eta+\epsilon_p),
\end{equation}
nearest-signature selection recovers the true candidate: its observation distance
is at most $\eta+\epsilon_p$, and triangle inequality puts every other candidate
strictly farther away. Continuous-parameter discretization additionally needs coverage
error. Finite search does not automatically guarantee global optimization/statistical
confidence. This describes a verifiable restricted structured mechanism, not identification
of arbitrary contact dynamics.

\textbf{The calibration-to-task gap.} Small probe errors do not imply the following
uniform joint-kernel error. Separately establish correct structure or validate error/
coverage on transport visitation support. Out-of-support probability and model bias
cannot be omitted. Independent validation thresholds are empirical rejection rules,
not probabilistic safety certificates.

\begin{proposition}[Calibration-to-response bridge within a structured family]
Let $d_\Theta$ be a distance on the declared mechanism family, possibly including
discrete delay, with true mechanism $\theta_\star$. Assume probe-model signatures satisfy
\[
 \|\hat z(\theta)-\hat z(\theta')\|\ge\kappa d_\Theta(\theta,\theta'),\qquad \kappa>0.
\]
If observation noise is at most $\eta$, probe-model bias at truth at most $\epsilon_p$,
and the selected estimate's actual fitting residual is
$r=\|\hat z(\hat\theta)-z_{\rm obs}\|$, then
\[
 d_\Theta(\hat\theta,\theta_\star)\le(r+\eta+\epsilon_p)/\kappa.
\]
Further assume joint model kernels are uniformly $L_\Theta$-Lipschitz in mechanisms
over common visitation support and all allowed transports, with uniform kernel bias
at truth at most $\epsilon_K$. The next section's error may then be bounded by
\[
 \varepsilon_{\mathcal B}\le\epsilon_K+\frac{L_\Theta}{\kappa}(r+\eta+\epsilon_p).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The first inequality follows from inverse separation and triangle inequality through
the observation. For the second, insert the model kernel at the true mechanism between
the actual kernel and estimated-model kernel, then apply triangle inequality. This
residual formulation does not require globally optimal fitting.
\end{proof}
This completes a \emph{conditional} chain: extrapolation from probes to transport
requires correct structured families/task support, separated signatures, and stable
parameter-to-joint-closed-loop response. Independently justify $\kappa,L_\Theta,\epsilon_K$;
finite validation cannot establish global bounds. Uniform Lipschitz conditions may
fail across contact events. Use local-mode bounds and the event margins below instead;
small continuous-gain errors are not whole-task guarantees.

\textbf{Contact events.} Suppose actual/predicted trajectories share a coupling with
expected trajectory distance at most $e$. For a success/failure event, let $M$ be
the actual trajectory's margin to the event boundary. For any $r>0$,
\begin{equation}
 |\Pr(A)-\Pr(\hat A)|\le e/r+\Pr(M\le r).
\end{equation}
Event disagreement requires distance at least $r$ or margin at most $r$; apply Markov
inequality. Near openings/force thresholds, the margin term may be large. Continuous
mean-response errors cannot replace event errors. Report contact establishment,
jamming, recovery, and overforce separately.

\subsection{Calibration Error and the Transport Objective}
Analyze short-horizon transport from a given initial state. Augmented state $x$ contains
target state/history, controller carry, event phase/tracking integral, source reference,
source/target skills, and modes. Actual/predicted rollouts use the same source reference
and transport sample. Source references are analysis/training-cost variables, not
unobserved target futures supplied online. Within a shared feasible parameter set
$\Phi$, assume all $\varphi$ have joint augmented closed-loop kernels satisfying uniformly
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{\varphi\in\Phi,\,x\in\mathcal X_\Phi}
 W_1(\mathbb K_t^\varphi(x),\widehat{\mathbb K}_t^\varphi(x))
 \le\varepsilon_{\mathcal B},
\end{equation}
with outputs including the task responses used. Support coverage and joint-kernel
$L$-Lipschitz constants are also uniform over $\Phi$. Target-marginal proximity alone
cannot control source-coupled cost. From identical augmented initial states,
\begin{equation}
 d_{k+1}\le Ld_k+\varepsilon_{\mathcal B},\qquad
 d_k\le\varepsilon_{\mathcal B}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}L^j.
 \label{eq:propagation}
\end{equation}
If horizon/mode-specific errors $\varepsilon_{k,m_k}$ and local propagation constants
$L_{k,m_k}$ are available only before the first contact-mode divergence, a more suitable
bound under the same coupling is
\begin{equation}
 d_K\le\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}\varepsilon_{j,m_j}
       \prod_{i=j+1}^{K-1}L_{i,m_i},
 \label{eq:mode-local-propagation}
\end{equation}
with empty products equal to $1$. Mode divergence still requires separate event-margin
control. Window-averaged response error cannot replace all $\varepsilon_{k,m_k}$;
uncalibrated ensemble disagreement is not their confidence bound. Split empirical
validation by horizon/contact event; reject or separately report unsupported requests.
For expected task cost plus source-coupled trajectory cost, uniform Lipschitz costs give
\begin{equation}
 |F_t(\varphi)-\widehat F_t(\varphi)|\le\varepsilon_F.
\end{equation}
Under these uniform conditions and shared feasibility, training to
$\varepsilon_{\rm opt}$ suboptimality in the model's short-horizon objective gives
\begin{equation}
 F_t(\hat\varphi)-\inf_{\varphi\in\Phi}F_t(\varphi)
 \le2\varepsilon_F+\varepsilon_{\rm opt}.
 \label{eq:objectivebound}
\end{equation}
This auxiliary guarantee concerns the specified transport objective, including source-
anchoring bias; it is not regret to the full target task-only optimal policy. CVaR,
rare events, and UOT extensions require separate analysis; real safety/general OT
guarantees do not follow directly.

\textbf{When improvement is guaranteed.} For baseline $\varphi_0$ in the same feasible
set, model improvement
$\widehat F_t(\varphi_0)-\widehat F_t(\hat\varphi)>2\varepsilon_F$
implies strict actual-cost improvement by subtracting uniform error bounds at both
ends. Smaller gaps do not guarantee superiority to direct transfer/system identification.
Randomness, flow, or the OT name alone supplies no improvement guarantee.

\subsection{Finite-Policy Evaluation Bounds}
A prefrozen finite policy set permits finite-sample cost comparison without claiming
validation of uniform-kernel assumptions over an infinite policy class. Let there
be $M$ policies and $n$ independently sampled post-calibration reset clusters, averaging
all prespecified modes within each cluster. Actual/model costs in a cluster use matched
random streams. Fix cost before evaluation, bounded in $[0,1]$, with observations
$a_{ji},p_{ji}$ and means $\bar a_i,\bar p_i$. Define
\[
 \beta_n=\sqrt{\frac{\log(4M/\alpha)}{2n}},\qquad
 \epsilon_n=\min\!\left\{1,\max_i\frac1n\sum_j|a_{ji}-p_{ji}|+
 \sqrt{\frac{\log(2M/\alpha)}{2n}}\right\}.
\]
\begin{proposition}[Cost-improvement bound for finitely many frozen policies]
If clusters are i.i.d. and independent of policy-selection data, then with probability
at least $1-\alpha$, simultaneously for all frozen policies $i$,
\[
 F_t(\varphi_0)-F_t(\varphi_i)
 \ge\bar p_0-\bar p_i-2\epsilon_n-2\beta_n.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Apply two-sided Hoeffding and a union bound to $M$ model means, and one-sided Hoeffding
and a union bound to $M$ bounded absolute paired errors, allocating $\alpha/2$ each.
Use $|F_t(\varphi_i)-\widehat F_t(\varphi_i)|\le\mathbb E|a_i-p_i|$ and triangle
inequality for baseline/policy endpoints.
\end{proof}
This is conditional on one calibration, the declared reset distribution, cost, and
finite policy set. Multiple calibrations need separate stratification/confidence
allocation. Small samples may yield a negative bound, requiring an uncertified result.
The bound does not cover unfrozen neural policies, unsupported contact, arbitrary
hardware noise, or unrecorded risks beyond the task-success indicator. It does not
guarantee wins against controllers optimizing other objectives.

\paragraph{Directly observed cost improvement.}
To validate frozen-policy effects without certifying the model, use a direct one-sided
bound on independent-reset paired cost differences
$d_{ji}=a_{j0}-a_{ji}\in[-1,1]$. For $M-1$ prespecified comparisons, with probability
at least $1-\alpha$, simultaneously
\[
 F_t(\varphi_0)-F_t(\varphi_i)
 \ge \bar d_i-\sqrt{\frac{2\log((M-1)/\alpha)}{n}}.
\]
Apply Hoeffding and a union bound to paired differences bounded over an interval of
length $2$. This certifies improvement in a fixed observed cost only, not calibration
identifiability, response-kernel accuracy, or cross-calibration statistics. Freeze
sample size/comparisons before executing these fresh resets; optional stopping is prohibited.

\section{Discussion}
Empirical contributions depend on established-method comparisons: improve constrained
task performance with identical target-data permissions, reduce interaction needed
for equal performance, or improve meaningful behavior choices/execution cost at
comparable task performance. Reduced source-response distance alone is insufficient.

If benefits arise only from interface-gain calibration, frame them as action adaptation.
If task-aware ASID matches performance, assess transport's increment carefully.
If ordinary response prediction matches the full method, narrow causal claims.
Target distributions need not globally copy source distributions; feasibility and
task outcomes should explain necessary mode changes.

\section{Limitations}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Generalization depends on structured mechanisms shared by calibration/tasks, shared-state sufficiency, and actual visitation support. Unknown hysteresis/contact branches may invalidate models.
\item Task geometry and source anchoring can bias learning; a transport-objective optimum is not an arbitrary target task-only optimum.
\item Composed short-horizon model error, rare-mode probabilities, and model-based CVaR need independent validation. Model-risk constraints are not hardware safety certificates.
\item Source-conditioned maps/flows do not guarantee preservation of all valid modes or creation of arbitrary new ones. Low-support regions need more interaction or broader priors.
\item Interventions identify responses under specified protocols, not unique latent DAGs or arbitrary individual cross-body counterfactuals.
\item Initial simulations use structured contact states; hardware platforms remain undecided. Results do not directly generalize to generic vision, arbitrary soft robots, or real bimanual transfer.
\item Task 4 depends on WBC with declared end-effector/force interfaces and shared source support. Local workcell success is not learned navigation, gait, or arbitrary-body whole-body manipulation. Body-specific feasibility/model error need separate acceptance.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
We aim to calibrate target execution mechanisms through limited interventions and
transform source task-conditioned behavior distributions into executable target
behavior. A complete first version includes source priors, explicit action decoding,
calibrated responses, amortized transport, distribution/closed-loop metrics, and
equal-budget established-method comparisons. Methods and experiments establish
contributions; theory explains their basis and boundaries.

\section{Supporting Derivations}
\subsection{Identifiability and Fixed-Design Uncertainty}
If two parameters yield identical calibration outputs, then
$v=\theta_1-\theta_2\in\ker A_{\mathcal B}$. Relevant responses agree iff $B_gv=0$,
directly giving the main-text null-space condition.

For fixed design and $\xi\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2I)$,
\begin{equation}
 \hat b_g=B_gA_{\mathcal B}^\dagger y,\qquad
 \Sigma_g=\sigma^2B_g(A_{\mathcal B}^\top A_{\mathcal B})^\dagger B_g^\top.
\end{equation}
Under identifiability, with probability at least $1-\eta$,
\begin{equation}
 \|\hat b_g-b_g\|\le
 \sqrt{\tr\Sigma_g}+\sqrt{2\|\Sigma_g\|_{\rm op}\log(1/\eta)}.
\end{equation}
Multiple queries require joint control. Real response models also need structural-
bias, mode-probability, and noise-law errors. Small mean error does not imply proximity
of the complete kernel.

\subsection{Propagation and Objective Error}
Apply triangle inequality to actual/predicted augmented-state laws sharing source references:
\begin{equation}
 d_{k+1}\le
 W_1(\mu_k\mathbb K_t^\varphi,\mu_k\widehat{\mathbb K}_t^\varphi)
 +W_1(\mu_k\widehat{\mathbb K}_t^\varphi,\hat\mu_k\widehat{\mathbb K}_t^\varphi)
 \le\varepsilon_{\mathcal B}+Ld_k.
\end{equation}
Trajectory costs require stepwise couplings, not merely marginal laws. Under corresponding
kernel couplings and uniform Lipschitz conditions,
\begin{equation}
 \varepsilon_F\le
 (L_C+\lambda_sL_c)\varepsilon_{\mathcal B}
 \sum_{k=1}^{H}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}L^j.
\end{equation}
Assume common source coupling, transport inputs, and actual/model feasibility. Without
valid risk tightening, model-feasible solutions need not be actually feasible.
UOT extensions/discontinuous events need separate error analysis.

Add/subtract model objectives to obtain the main-text bound:
\begin{align}
 F_t(\hat\varphi)-F_t(\varphi^*)
 &= [F_t(\hat\varphi)-\widehat F_t(\hat\varphi)]
 +[\widehat F_t(\hat\varphi)-\widehat F_t(\varphi^*)]
 +[\widehat F_t(\varphi^*)-F_t(\varphi^*)]\\
 &\le2\varepsilon_F+\varepsilon_{\rm opt}.
\end{align}
The optimized quantity is the specified transport objective. Do not remove source
regularization and reinterpret it as unconstrained RL regret.

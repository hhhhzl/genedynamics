\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
Experiments address three questions: task/resource tradeoffs of the complete method
against established methods; mechanisms and applicability of calibration and selective
response transfer; and executability of a shared skill interface across bodies.
T1--T3 provide the main manipulation-performance comparisons, while T4 extends morphology.
Every method reports all four tasks. This section specifies the proposed next-round
protocol; formal tables and conclusions await independent evaluation.

\subsection{Tasks, Bodies, and Shared Control Interface}
The source robot is Panda, the fixed-arm target is xArm7, and UR5e tests transfer
across degrees of freedom. Target geometry, joint limits, tools, and control capabilities
are public to all methods. Unknown control responses and contact parameters are learned
through declared target interaction. Cross body and mechanism changes independently:
same body/different response, different bodies/similar response, and simultaneous changes.

All four tasks use task-space skills $U\in\R^{H\times13}$:
\begin{equation}
 U_j=\big[v_j^\top,\ \omega_j^\top,
 (\log k^{\rm trans}_j)^\top,
 (\log k^{\rm rot}_j)^\top,\ F^d_{n,j}\big]^\top.
 \label{eq:experimentalprimitive}
\end{equation}
Here $v,\omega$ are linear/angular velocities, $k$ denotes diagonal stiffness relative
to reference values, and $F_n^d$ is the normal-force setpoint. Each body executes these
commands through its own low-level interface; force commands are masked in free space.
Determine thresholds, frames, control periods, collision allowlists, termination rules,
and initial-state distributions during development, then freeze them before formal evaluation.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{Task \arabic*.},leftmargin=2.1em]
\item \textbf{ObstacleTransfer.} Carry an orientation-sensitive rigid object around
beams and side panels, through a pose-constrained 3D opening, to a specified $SE(3)$
pose. Source behavior includes overpass and side-route paths. Evaluate how geometric
differences, execution delay, and payload changes affect collision-free completion,
braking, and tracking.
\item \textbf{GuidedContactAlignment.} Establish contact with a 3D guide surface and
slide/rotate into an alignment region. Vary control response, friction, and compliance;
test whether pressure, speed, stiffness, and contact sequence still need adaptation
when free-space tracking is similar.
\item \textbf{KeyedInsertionRecovery.} Start with lateral/angular offsets and insert
an asymmetric connector into a matching socket, including bounded search, retraction,
realignment, and reinsertion. Evaluate overall completion, post-jam recovery, and
instantaneous lateral forces rather than average contact force alone.
\item \textbf{MobileKeyedInsertionRecovery.} Preserve Task 3's object, fixture, force
limits, and recovery semantics while changing body-support/contact coupling. Test
constrained realization of fixed-arm skills on mobile manipulators and legged platforms
with arms.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Task 4: Same Operation with Mobile Support}
\label{sec:task4}
Task 4 reuses the frozen Panda Task 3 prior. First compare the same arm with fixed
and mobile support, then test legged bodies with arms. Define actual wheel/ground or
foot/ground dynamics and platform constraints separately. Planar-motion proxies are
interface diagnostics only. Claims across mobile-support types require independent
evidence from multiple valid support types. Humanoid inclusion depends on available
control interfaces and is not assumed complete.

All high-level methods on one body share WBC, stance rules, and feasibility projection.
Task conditions include available base pose/velocity, support contacts, joint margins,
and controller history; body-specific state is not inserted directly into the frozen
Panda prior. End-effector response correspondence permits legal body motion needed
for task completion, while body stability enters target constraints. UMI-on-Legs
provides a reference for manipulation-policy/whole-body-control hierarchy\cite{umilegs}.
Report system comparisons changing WBC separately from high-level comparisons with fixed WBC.

\subsection{Strong Baselines and Data Permissions}
\textbf{Controlled calibration protocol A.} All methods receive identical source data,
public task goals, known body models, available sensing, and low-level interfaces.
The target provides only budgeted short probes, without complete successful task
demonstrations or target online RL. Post-calibration simulated training uses only models
built from public models and budgeted records. The hidden target environment supplies
charged probes and sealed evaluation; its actual mechanism parameters are unavailable
for training.

\textbf{Four-task main table.} The planned roster has nine rows: ours, same-capacity
matched task-only, task-adapted FADA, task-adapted DIAL-MPC, ordinary response MPC,
public geometry/feedback, public source prior, task-adapted AdaptSim, and task-adapted
ASID. Each row retains a position in all four tasks. Fill metrics only with a verifiable
task actor, training/target-access ledger, and physical execution under the common
contract. Missing assets, unfinished adaptations, or predeployment disqualification
are explicitly unexecuted, not zero success used to support superiority. Same-capacity
task-only appears in both the main table and mechanism ablations; it tests transport's
increment and does not replace established external comparisons. The roster combines
recent complete methods, identification/simulation adaptation, and strong public
control\cite{fada,dialmpc,adaptsim,asid}.

Incomplete task adaptations of ACP and Human2Sim2Robot remain outside-table diagnostics;
unexecuted requests are not zero-scored\cite{acp,human2sim}. Play2Perfect, ASAP,
SPI-Active, and XMoP remain related/future routes rather than incomplete main-table

and common-interface adaptation are distinct; report source, known-nominal, and target
funnels for every task-adapted row.

\textbf{Valid implementations and predeployment rejection.} Methods require correct
action/data interfaces, faithful core training mechanisms, and reasonable training/tuning
opportunities. Quality checks do not require success on every nominal request. Missing
trained policies or unfinished adaptations are incomplete, not synthetic zeros supporting
superiority. Deployment rejections by evaluable algorithms remain in the full request
denominator, with capped time one for unfinished requests. Report rejection rates and
physical execution counts separately. Physical violations come only from executed
trajectories; unexecuted requests do not automatically violate physical limits. Retain
and explain actual low success and failures.

DR/recurrent PPO, classical Residual RL, generic calibrated MPC, and Mirage remain
foundational references\cite{dr,ppo,rppo,residualrl,mjpc,mirage}. FORGE is a RA-L 2025
contact reference, not a verified recent main-conference method\cite{forge}.
Same-capacity task-only, deterministic transport, reweighting-only, and removal of
response/mode/risk terms are internal ablations. Strong controls may use continuous
actions, skill chunks, history conditioning, and reasonable network capacity; they
are not restricted to a finite mapping-candidate library.

\textbf{Complete adaptation under common permissions.} B-log gives ours and controls
such as FADA/ASAP complete self-collected action/observation trajectories under identical
total duration, measurable information, resets, and execution constraints, without
target rewards or expert demonstrations. Each method chooses its collection strategy.
An additional shared-record-buffer control separates collection from adaptation gains.
List ASAP's state alignment and additional measurement permissions separately; methods
within a group receive the same available information. Omitting rewards does not turn
complete target trajectories into protocol A short probes. B-online permits charged
target-task interaction and reward. C-supervised supplies our extended version with
the same available demonstrations and labels supervision. Native systems with different
information report performance--cost frontiers separately rather than equal-budget
rankings. External pretraining is listed separately. Full systems may compare performance
with declared resources; differing permission-label strings alone do not invalidate
comparison. Information equivalence depends on actual data/model provenance. Online
optimization on a common model mainly changes computation, measured by online latency,
model calls, and deadline misses. D-oracle is sealed diagnosis with true target parameters.
Report separate data curves for latent alignment using large random target datasets,
diffusion/latent-action policies trained on target demonstrations, and target online
residual RL\cite{lsa,lad}. Extensively target-trained policies and ground-truth planners
are excluded from the small-calibration main table and are not proven performance bounds.

\textbf{Interface and training fairness.} Controlled comparisons share sensing,
available history, public task reward, control period, command bounds, and safety
criteria. Privileged critics may access only their training model's state, never hidden
target parameters. Record native reproduction checks separately from common-task-interface
adaptations; an identically named repository policy is not automatically a paper reproduction.

\subsection{Budget, Generalization, and Statistical Evaluation}
Report target access in seconds, transitions, resets, and data types. A short-probe
subprotocol may use $B\in\{0,5,10,30\}$; freeze duration and fit/validation allocation
before formal testing. Methods such as FADA requiring roughly two minutes of action/
observation logs also receive a native-duration point\cite{fada}; an unsuitable tiny
buffer cannot represent full performance. Declare whether target trajectories are
complete task executions even without rewards; list native demonstrations separately.
Charge interface adjustments, validation, and failed attempts. Active methods share
the same total target-access budget.

List simulated training transitions, offline updates, tuning attempts, GPU time,
and deployment-time model calls separately. Compare learning curves at matched simulated
data and performance at fixed latency. Update counts across optimizers are not equal
compute. Account for MPC overruns using a prespecified execution rule.

Hold out initial states, mechanism combinations, and bodies separately. Distinguish
UR5e protocols: frozen transport with context fitting only, versus few-shot adaptation
allowing policy updates in the calibrated model. Training on known target morphology
is not frozen generalization to unseen bodies. Report simulation and hardware separately;
do not presuppose hardware conclusions before platforms/sensing interfaces are defined.

Formal evaluation freezes eight independent training/calibration instances, two target
mechanism groups, and paired requests. Tasks 1--3 use $10$ distinct physical initial
states $\times2$ requested modes $\times2$ mechanism groups: $40$ requests/instance.
Task 4 additionally crosses two body/support cells: $80$ requests. Deterministic public
controllers retain one method identity and execute the same request package, without
fabricated eight-fold training replication. All methods share task/mechanism/body/initial/
mode keys; failures are not replaced. Complete post-reset physics/controller-state
hashes define physical initial conditions. Episode labels or policy seeds on identical
deterministic resets do not create new initial states.

The eight-instance count is frozen through prespecified paired-discordance power
simulation on development data. Moderate discordance reaches target power; high training
heterogeneity may remain underpowered and is reported as a limitation, without adding
instances after final-result inspection. Inference uses training/calibration instance
as the highest random level, followed by mechanism/body/initial request blocks in
$10{,}000$ hierarchical paired bootstrap samples. Prespecify one primary contrast per
RQ and apply Holm correction within each RQ family. Report effects, intervals, and
raw numerators/denominators, without an unjustified overall cross-task ranking. All
support failures, validation rejections, overforce, timeouts, and body failures enter
the overall denominator.

\subsection{Performance Evidence and Contribution Tests}
The four-task main table reports constrained success, physical-constraint violation,
capped normalized completion time with failures set to one, and worst-mechanism-group
constrained success. T1--T3 receive equal-weight macro-averaging; T4 is a separate
morphology extension. At saturated success, still compare completion time/risk;
do not report speed only on mutually successful episodes. List target interaction,
training compute, and online latency separately. Different permissions do not support
same-information causal ranking. Dense reward trains policies; common task-success
criteria evaluate them. Cumulative rewards cannot replace cross-task success/safety.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Interaction efficiency.} Under equal target-access permissions, compare
success--budget curves and target interaction needed to reach a prespecified success level.
\item \textbf{Contact and recovery.} Under common constraints, compare contact alignment,
overall post-jam recovery, force peaks, and failure probabilities, including unseen
mechanism combinations.
\item \textbf{Task--behavior tradeoff.} First control task cost, then compare meaningful
mode coverage, responses, and conditional correspondence. Without mode requests, let
baselines choose more reliable modes. With explicit requests, provide identical
conditioning and evaluate requested-mode and autonomous completion separately.
\item \textbf{Amortized execution.} At task performance comparable to calibrated-model
MPC, test whether transport reduces end-to-end online latency, including WBC,
state processing, and model computation.
\end{enumerate}
Reduced source--target response distance, added noise, or larger models alone do not
constitute performance contributions. Validity depends on these comparisons; superiority
on every task/metric cannot be presumed. Before new tests, fix the task set, effects,
and comparison families for full-system comparisons. Evaluate overall tradeoffs using
equal-weight T1--T3 metrics and per-task results, with T4 separately reporting morphology.
Ties or small regressions on some tasks do not automatically negate gains elsewhere,
but risks, failures, and uncertainty must be fully reported. Task-only ablation explains
response terms in specific conditions; it need not show per-request dominance or wins
on all four tasks. Public execution, continued training, and checkpoint selection may
improve the complete system, but only ablations matching those factors attribute
differences specifically to source response or gating. Conditional benefits must be
claimed only within the evaluated conditions.

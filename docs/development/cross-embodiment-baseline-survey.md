# Cross-Embodiment: Screening Recent Strong Baselines from Leading Conferences

Search date: 2026-10-14. This document screens the literature and reproduction eligibility. No third-party environments were installed, none of these methods were run, and no new experimental results are included. See the [four-environment comparison plan](cross-embodiment-baseline-comparison-plan.md) for the overall comparison protocol. The paper draft contains only literature positioning, methods, and the formal evaluation design; reproduction procedures and experiment reports remain in the development directory and result artifacts.

The user requested mature, complete, strong baselines, prioritizing leading conferences from the past two years and work by **C. Karen Liu and Guanya Shi**. The first round starts from complete methods in specific papers. DR/PPO, older residual RL, and generic MuJoCo MPC remain basic references rather than the main comparisons against recent literature.

Reproduction, development comparisons, method improvements, and formal validation are now separated into P3–P7; see the
[phase execution plan](cross-embodiment-p3-onward-plan.md). The initial method pool is separate from the actual execution queue:
routes with unavailable assets remain pending while other native qualification work proceeds. This does not constitute complete reproduction.

## 1. Conference Years and Reproduction Eligibility

- The main pool prioritizes **2025/2026 main-conference papers at CoRL, RSS, ICRA, IROS, ICLR, and NeurIPS**, with formal proceedings or verifiable main-conference acceptance. Journals, workshops, and submissions under review are distinguished explicitly.
- The rolling two-year window is **2024-10-14 through 2026-10-14**. The conference dates of CoRL 2024 and NeurIPS 2024 may still qualify, but these are boundary candidates only. Use the conference year: publication of CoRL 2024 proceedings in 2025 does not make a paper CoRL 2025.
- CoRL 2026 entries corroborated by author acceptance announcements, laboratory pages, and official repositories are labeled **“accepted; verified through author announcements; formal proceedings pending verification.”** Do not claim the conference has already taken place or use an arXiv year as evidence of acceptance.
- Having a repository does not establish that the complete system is runnable. Verify training, adaptation, data/assets, checkpoints, evaluation, and deployment interfaces separately. Resources that have not been obtained or executed must not be described as reproduced.
- When native embodiments, sensors, actions, or controllers differ, use `*-adapted` and retain a change log. Removing a core stage prevents the original method name from representing the complete system.

## 3. Work by Karen Liu

### 3.1 Human2Sim2Robot: Initial Cross-Embodiment Route

**Crossing the Human-Robot Embodiment Gap with Sim-to-Real RL using One Human Demonstration**, Tyler Ga Wei Lum, Olivia Y. Lee, **C. Karen Liu**, and Jeannette Bohg; **CoRL 2025**. [Formal proceedings](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v305/lum25a.html), [official project](https://human2sim2robot.github.io/), [official code](https://github.com/tylerlum/human2sim2robot). The original method extracts object trajectories and pre-manipulation hand poses from a single human RGB-D video, trains RL in a digital twin, and uses no real-robot policy fine-tuning. Training and processing code is released; downloading data, assets, and models requires the authors' form, and these have not yet been obtained or validated by execution.

**Adaptation assessment.** Prioritize T1/T2; T3 may undergo qualification. The native setting is dexterous KUKA + Allegro manipulation, without native validation for fixed-tool insertion or mobile WBC. Replacing human video with source-robot trajectories requires the label `Human2Sim2Robot-adapted`, retaining object-trajectory rewards and pre-manipulation initialization. Versions using successful demonstrations belong to **C-supervised**. A source-only adaptation requires a separate equal-information subprotocol: both sides share identical source trajectories, nominal/calibrated target models, and supervision. It cannot be called a native demonstration-free method.

### 3.2 Play2Perfect: Qualification Priority for Precise Assembly

**Play2Perfect: What Matters in Dexterous Play Pretraining for Precise Assembly?**, Tyler Ga Wei Lum, Kushal Kedia, **C. Karen Liu**, and Jeannette Bohg; **accepted at CoRL 2026**, according to the [first author's homepage](https://tylerlum.github.io/) and [Stanford IPRL acceptance announcement/list](https://iprl.stanford.edu/); formal proceedings remain to be verified. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26428), [project](https://play2perfect.github.io/), [official code](https://github.com/kushal2000/play2perfect). The original method retains large-scale simulated dexterous-play pretraining followed by simulation RL fine-tuning for assembly CAD/tasks, with zero real-robot fine-tuning. The repository releases both training stages, assembly evaluation, checkpoints, and deployment references.

**Adaptation assessment.** Prioritize T3, then T2, without forcing coverage of T1/T4. The native system uses a KUKA arm and a 22-DoF Sharpa hand; in-hand object reorientation and play pretraining are central. If the current fixed grasp eliminates these capabilities entirely, leaving ordinary RL fine-tuning, it no longer represents complete Play2Perfect. First assess which core elements can be retained, or use an extended dexterous task. The paper uses prolonged pretraining on a single RTX A6000; measure resources on the current 24 GB GPU before promising runtimes. External checkpoints/play data belong to the **external-pretraining column of C-supervised**; simulation training from scratch is reported separately.

### 3.3 SimToolReal: Alternative for Object-Centric Tool Generalization

**SimToolReal: An Object-Centric Policy for Zero-Shot Dexterous Tool Manipulation**, Kushal Kedia, Tyler Ga Wei Lum, Jeannette Bohg, and **C. Karen Liu**; **RSS 2026**. [Formal proceedings](https://roboticsproceedings.org/rss22/p151.html), [official code](https://github.com/tylerlum/simtoolreal). The original method trains a general object-goal-pose policy using procedurally generated tools; deployment uses object pose/geometry and target trajectories, without tool- or task-specific target training. Training, DexToolBench, checkpoints, and deployment code are released; distinguish the paper's Isaac Gym recipe from the currently recommended Isaac Sim route.

**Adaptation assessment.** T1/T2 can test object-centric generalization; T3 is a difficult-transfer reference. A fixed tool removes native dexterous-hand capabilities and requires explicit disclosure. Together with Play2Perfect, it can form a question sequence from zero-shot tool generalization to precise-assembly fine-tuning, without making both main baselines. T4 is mismatched. External models and pretraining volume are listed separately.

### 3.4 Cloak: Alternative for a Visual Protocol

**Cloak: Zero-Shot Cross-Embodiment Manipulation by Masking the End-Effector from the VLA**, Michael Piseno, Guy Tevet, and **C. Karen Liu**; **accepted at CoRL 2026**, according to the [official Stanford project](https://tml.stanford.edu/cloak/). Independent reading of the [OpenReview entry](https://openreview.net/forum?id=7KV78k8rcv) has not been completed. The [official code](https://github.com/Stanford-TML/cloak) provides training, deployment, processing, testing, and checkpoint entry points. The original method addresses appearance and kinematic differences through end-effector geometry masking and fingertip FK/IK, relying on VLA and robot-data pretraining.

**Adaptation assessment.** Suitable for a T1 visual extension. Extracting only FK/IK for the current state/force protocol cannot be called complete Cloak; resources such as π0.5/DROID belong to **C-supervised**. Whether to add visual comparisons should follow the paper's research question, not expand implementation scope merely for an author or year.

## 4. Work by Guanya Shi

### 4.1 FADA: Initial Route for Execution Alignment from Limited Data

**FADA: Few-Shot Domain Adaptation via Dynamics Alignment for Humanoid Control**, Angchen Xie, Nikhil Sobanbabu, Ishayu Shikhare, Alan Wang, Max Simchowitz, and **Guanya Shi**; **accepted at CoRL 2026**, according to the [first author's September 2026 announcement](https://angchenxie.github.io/), [official LeCAR listing](https://lecar-lab.github.io/publications.html), and [official code](https://github.com/LeCAR-Lab/FADA); formal proceedings remain to be verified. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28476). The original method trains a PPO oracle and distills it into Planner-IDM through DAgger. It freezes the planner and supervises IDM fine-tuning using approximately **120 seconds of target actions/observations**, without target rewards or expert demonstrations. Main training, LoRA fine-tuning, MuJoCo/hardware collection, and G1/T1 oracle checkpoints are released; whole-body tracking has not been released.

**Adaptation assessment.** A direct competitor to preserving intent while adapting execution. T1–T3 require new arm/task oracles, Planner-IDM, and action interfaces. T4 is closer to the original work, but a humanoid checkpoint cannot directly control Go2 + Z1. `FADA-adapted` retains the frozen planner, IDM learning, and reward-free target adaptation. Native, complete, self-collected action/observation trajectories belong to **B-log**, including at least a data point near 120 seconds. The absence of reward or matching probe duration does not make this A-fixed. A version using only prescribed short probes is an **A-fixed adaptation**, requiring excitation-coverage validation and separate reporting; it does not represent the benefits of native collection. Privileged state for oracle training must come only from the public training model, never the hidden target mechanism.

### 4.2 ASAP: Second-Batch Simulator Alignment and Retraining

**ASAP: Aligning Simulation and Real-World Physics for Learning Agile Humanoid Whole-Body Skills**, Tairan He et al., including **Guanya Shi**; **RSS 2025**. [Formal proceedings/full author list](https://roboticsproceedings.org/rss21/p066.html), [official code](https://github.com/LeCAR-Lab/ASAP). The original method collects target trajectories, learns a delta-action model, and fine-tunes the source policy in a simulator augmented with that model; deployment omits the delta model. Training, delta-model learning, fine-tuning, action data, and MuJoCo/hardware workflows are public.

**Adaptation assessment.** T4 is closest to the original work. Arm/contact versions for T1–T3 must be labeled `ASAP-adapted`, preserving the full alignment → policy fine-tuning chain. Native complete target action/state trajectories belong to **B-log**, with full-state alignment and measurement access declared; hardware MoCap or similar measurements cannot be treated as free common observations. A version sharing only prescribed short probes is an **A-fixed adaptation**, reported separately. Additional supervision such as expert tracking demonstrations is separately annotated **C-supervised**. Simulation policy fine-tuning does not imply querying target rewards. Fitting only gain/delay cannot be renamed ASAP, and target collection or simulation fine-tuning volume must not be hidden.

### 4.3 SPI-Active: Task-Specific Active Identification for T4

**Sampling-based System Identification with Active Exploration for Legged Sim2Real Learning**, Nikhil Sobanbabu, Guanqi He, Tairan He, Yuxiang Yang, and **Guanya Shi**; **CoRL 2025 Oral**, with the title taken from the [formal proceedings](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v305/sobanbabu25a.html). [Official code](https://github.com/LeCAR-Lab/SPI-Active), [active-identification instructions](https://github.com/LeCAR-Lab/SPI-Active/blob/main/active_sysid.md). The original chain is parallel sampling-based identification → Fisher-information active exploration → re-identification → downstream RL. Main training modules are released, but sim2real, data replay, and connections to active collection remain incomplete; resources cannot be called complete end to end.

**Adaptation assessment.** Prioritize Go2 mass/CoM/inertia/motor changes in T4. Fixed-arm contact requires new parameters and executable excitation. The complete system belongs to **A-active**, charging all target duration, failures, resets, and transitions. Identification from a fixed buffer is only an ablation and cannot represent native active-exploration gains. An arm-only model cannot support conclusions about mobile-body coupling.

### 4.4 DIAL-MPC: Recent Complete Model-Based Planning Reference

**Full-Order Sampling-Based MPC for Torque-Level Locomotion Control via Diffusion-Style Annealing**, Haoru Xue, Chaoyi Pan, Zeji Yi, Guannan Qu, and **Guanya Shi**; **ICRA 2025**, held 2025-05-19–23. [IEEE proceedings](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11127320/), [official awards page](https://2025.ieee-icra.org/program/awards-and-finalists/), [official code](https://github.com/LeCAR-Lab/dial-mpc). The original method requires no policy training and uses full-order dynamics for online sampling-based MPC, with Brax/MJX environments, a real-time mode, and Go2 deployment.

**Adaptation assessment.** After model calibration from shared probes, retain continuous trajectories, warm starts, and sufficient planning; classify it as **A-fixed** with an additional online-computation budget. T1–T3 require arm/contact models and task costs. If native whole-body torque control replaces WBC in T4, report a separate system comparison; optimizing references above the same WBC is labeled `DIAL-MPC-planner-adapted`. Report model calls per step, latency, and deadline misses. Ground-truth models belong only to **D-oracle**.

**MBD boundary candidate.** [Model-based Diffusion for Trajectory Optimization, NeurIPS 2024](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/6a5c38a730b17f3827c09cf6b192be04-Abstract-Conference.html), also coauthored by Guanya Shi, and its [official code](https://github.com/LeCAR-Lab/model-based-diffusion) can serve as an algorithmic source for DIAL-MPC or an offline-planning reference. Its conference date is within the rolling window. Do not directly mix original open-loop results with closed-loop comparisons; both need not be core methods simultaneously.

## 7. Data Access and Fairness

| Protocol | Available target data/information | Method assignment and comparison |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A-fixed** | Identical charged short-probe buffer and known geometry/interfaces; no complete target trajectories, successful demonstrations, or reward rollouts | Short-probe adaptations of FADA/ASAP are separate entries; Human2Sim2Robot adaptation with shared source trajectories; DIAL-MPC with a calibrated model. Fixed data does not restrict model training or planning capacity. |
| **A-active** | Self-selected probes, with target duration, counts, failures, resets, and transitions charged consistently | Complete SPI-Active exploration → identification → downstream training. Do not claim its buffer is identical to A-fixed. |
| **B-log** | Charged complete self-collected action/observation trajectories, without target reward or expert demonstrations; extra state measurements separately labeled | Native FADA collection access and complete ASAP trajectory/alignment access. Compare interaction duration and coverage; equal seconds do not justify ranking alongside short probes. |
| **B-online** | Charged complete target-task rollouts/rewards, without free extra demonstrations | SPARR/online-RL methods. Plot performance against target-interaction seconds/transitions, separately from A/B-log. |
| **C-supervised** | Demonstrations, corrections, paired actions, external pretraining/checkpoints itemized | Human2Sim2Robot, ACP, Cloak, LAD, and external XMoP/Play2Perfect priors. Source-only adaptations receive separate subprotocols and are not mathematical upper bounds. |
| **D-oracle** | Sealed target parameters, ground-truth planning, or high-budget feasibility search | Post-freeze explanation of mechanisms/feasibility, with no training feedback and no equal-budget ranking. |

Target interaction is measured primarily in **seconds**, alongside episodes/resets, transitions, and failed attempts. B-log must cover FADA's native data scale of approximately 120 seconds. Explicitly record whether data comes from prescribed probes or full-task rollouts: no reward does not automatically satisfy short-probe restrictions, and equal interaction seconds do not imply a shared ranking. Human2Sim2Robot's one demonstration, FADA's two minutes of reward-free records, and Play2Perfect's zero real-robot fine-tuning are different resource claims.

External methods retain mature architectures, historical feedback, continuous actions, chunks, full planning, and necessary training volume. Only internal Full/task-only comparisons require matched capacity. Share success/risk criteria, target-access permissions, available information, and compatible low-level interfaces; report simulation transitions, update counts, network capacity, source pretraining, GPU time, and deployment latency separately. If native vision is outside the common observation set, use a separate system/extra-observation comparison. State-only adaptations must not impersonate the complete original method. Separate nominal-target-body training from frozen unseen-body generalization; hidden target mechanisms cannot be free actor/model inputs.

## 8. How the Four Environments Test the Paper's Added Value

| Environment | Recent-method roles | Main performance | Contribution question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **T1 ObstacleTransfer** | Human2Sim2Robot route, FADA-adapted; XMoP-specific comparison | Collision-free completion for the full arm/carried object, SE(3) error, clearance, duration, latency | After object-centric training, execution alignment, and strong geometric planning, is the method more reliable or more target-data efficient? |
| **T2 GuidedContactAlignment** | ACP, FADA/ASAP-adapted; Human2Sim2Robot contact transfer | Force-limited positioning, contact maintenance/release, slip/stall, force peaks and duration | What does response calibration add beyond mature compliance and history feedback? |
| **T3 KeyedInsertionRecovery** | ACP; qualification of the Play2Perfect core; FADA/ASAP and SPARR separated by budget | Full-denominator success, jam recovery, excessive force, retries, rejection/timeout, modes and duration | After complete pretraining/fine-tuning and contact learning, do calibration and recovery have independent value? |
| **T4 MobileKeyedInsertionRecovery** | SPI-Active, FADA/ASAP body interfaces; DIAL-MPC system comparison | Joint manipulation/support success, slip, body displacement, WBC saturation, end-to-end latency | Does coupled identification outperform arm-only identification, and can high-level method contributions be separated from controller contributions? |

Not every method must natively cover all four environments. Common adaptations support cross-task comparisons; specialized methods provide strong controls in their suitable tasks, and complete systems that replace WBC receive separate tables. The current standing Go2 + Z1/planar-caster proxy in T4 is not humanoid locomotion; effective support dynamics and interfaces require qualification first.

Paper contributions follow preregistered metrics: reliability under equal target access, interaction required to reach equal performance, generalization to unseen mechanisms/bodies, and online cost at comparable task performance. Mode/response fidelity is an additional contribution only when task costs meet preregistered tolerances and the modes have task meaning. Rejections and timeouts remain in the full denominator; do not report only the accepted subset.

Before implementation, freeze a reproduction contract for each method: paper/code versions and licenses, acquired assets, native stages, interface changes, budget group, training/selection sets, and independent evaluation. First establish a minimal native closed loop and project-specific qualification, then development budget curves, and finally freeze formal experiments around the incremental contribution questions. Do not promise superiority to every recent method in advance.

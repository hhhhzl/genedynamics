\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:prelim}

\subsection{Lower-Control Optimal Control}
\label{subsec:prelim_lower_control}

We consider a robot with state $s_t\in\mathcal S$ and a generalized lower-control input
$u_t\in\mathcal U$. In contrast to trajectory-level planning, the sampled variable in this work is
not a state trajectory, but a finite-horizon lower-control sequence
\begin{equation}
    U
    =
    u_{0:H-1}
    \in \mathcal U^H .
\end{equation}
The state sequence is obtained by rolling out the closed-loop dynamics
\begin{equation}
    s_{h+1}
    =
    F(s_h,u_h;c),
    \qquad h=0,\ldots,H-1,
    \label{eq:closed_loop_rollout}
\end{equation}
where $c$ denotes the task context, including the goal, environment geometry, contact mode, object
state, terrain information, and robot-specific model parameters. The map $F$ is a closed-loop
lower-control transition. If $u_h$ is a torque command, then $F(s_h,u_h;c)=f(s_h,u_h)$.
If $u_h$ is a position--stiffness primitive, then
\begin{equation}
    a_h=\pi_{\mathrm{low}}(s_h,u_h;c),
    \qquad
    F(s_h,u_h;c)=f(s_h,a_h),
\end{equation}
where $\pi_{\mathrm{low}}$ can be an impedance controller, whole-body controller, or tracking
controller. Therefore, the same formulation covers torque-level control, impedance-level control,
and reference-level whole-body control.

The lower-control finite-horizon objective is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    J_{\mathrm{cl}}(U;s_0,c)
    =
    \ell_T(s_H,c)
    +
    \sum_{h=0}^{H-1}
    \ell(s_h,u_h,c),
    \qquad
    s_{h+1}=F(s_h,u_h;c).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:closed_loop_cost}
\end{equation}
The corresponding constrained lower-control problem is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    U^\star
    \in
    \arg\min_U
    \quad&
    J_{\mathrm{cl}}(U;s_0,c)
    \\
    \mathrm{s.t.}\quad&
    s_{h+1}=F(s_h,u_h;c),
    \\
    &
    h(s_h,u_h,c)=0,
    \\
    &
    g(s_h,u_h,c)\le 0,
    \qquad h=0,\ldots,H-1.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:lower_control_ocp}
\end{equation}
Here $h$ encodes equality constraints, such as contact consistency, surface attachment, foot-ground
contact, and object motion constraints. The inequality constraints $g$ encode safety and viability
constraints, such as friction cones, force limits, joint limits, collision avoidance, balance margins,
and tipping margins.

\subsection{Position--Stiffness Control Primitive}
\label{subsec:prelim_position_stiffness}

In this work, the lower-control input $u_h$ is not restricted to joint torque. We primarily consider
position--stiffness control primitives of the form
\begin{equation}
    u_h
    =
    (r_h,K_h,\nu_h),
    \label{eq:position_stiffness_u}
\end{equation}
where $r_h$ denotes a task-space, contact-space, or whole-body reference, $K_h$ denotes a stiffness
matrix, and $\nu_h$ collects additional feedforward terms such as desired contact force, desired
velocity, or footstep parameters. The stiffness must remain positive definite,
\begin{equation}
    K_h\in\mathbb S_{++}^{d}.
\end{equation}
We use a log-Euclidean parametrization
\begin{equation}
    K_h=\exp(S_h),
    \qquad
    S_h=S_h^\top,
    \label{eq:stiffness_log_param}
\end{equation}
so that sampling and optimization are performed over the symmetric matrix $S_h$, while the
executed stiffness $K_h$ is positive definite by construction.

For example, in surface-contact manipulation, the primitive can be
\begin{equation}
    u_h^{\mathrm{arm}}
    =
    (\Delta \xi_h,\Delta \eta_h,\Delta\psi_h,S_h,F_{n,h}^{d}),
\end{equation}
where $(\xi,\eta)$ are surface coordinates, $\psi$ is the yaw angle around the surface normal,
$S_h$ parametrizes the probe stiffness, and $F_{n,h}^{d}$ is the desired normal force. In humanoid
loco-manipulation, the primitive can be
\begin{equation}
    u_h^{\mathrm{hum}}
    =
    (v_h^{\mathrm{base}},p_h^{\mathrm{foot}},c_h^{\mathrm{hand}},
    S_h^{\mathrm{hand}},S_h^{\mathrm{body}},F_{n,h}^{d}),
\end{equation}
where $c_h^{\mathrm{hand}}$ denotes a hand-object contact coordinate, such as a face index and local
surface coordinates on the object.

\subsection{Diffusion-Style Control over Control Sequences}
\label{subsec:prelim_diffusion_control}

Following diffusion-style sampling-based control, we treat the lower-control sequence $U$ as the
diffusion variable. Let $U_1$ denote a clean control sequence and $U_k$ denote its noisy version at
reverse step $k$. The forward noising process is
\begin{equation}
    U_k
    =
    \sqrt{\bar\alpha_k}U_1
    +
    \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_k}\epsilon,
    \qquad
    \epsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,I),
    \label{eq:forward_noising_U}
\end{equation}
where $\bar\alpha_k=\prod_{j=1}^k\alpha_j$. The noisy marginal is
\begin{equation}
    p_k(U_k)
    =
    \int q(U_k\mid U_1)p_1(U_1)dU_1.
\end{equation}
A score-based reverse update uses
\begin{equation}
    S_k(U_k)=\nabla_{U_k}\log p_k(U_k).
\end{equation}
In model-free diffusion, $S_k$ is learned from data. In model-based diffusion, the score can be
estimated through Monte Carlo evaluation of clean control proposals. Given a noisy control sequence
$U_k$, the forward kernel induces the clean proposal distribution
\begin{equation}
    \Omega_k(U_k)
    =
    \mathcal N
    \left(
    \frac{U_k}{\sqrt{\bar\alpha_k}},
    \left(\frac{1}{\bar\alpha_k}-1\right)\Sigma_k
    \right).
    \label{eq:clean_proposal_U}
\end{equation}
For candidate controls $\tilde U_{k,m}\sim\Omega_k(U_k)$, the state trajectory is not sampled
independently, but obtained by rollout:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde s_{h+1}^{m}
    =
    F(\tilde s_h^{m},\tilde u_{k,m,h};c).
    \label{eq:sample_rollout_U_to_s}
\end{equation}
The clean estimate is computed by likelihood weighting,
\begin{equation}
    \bar U_k
    =
    \frac{
    \sum_{m=1}^{M} w_{k,m}\tilde U_{k,m}
    }{
    \sum_{m=1}^{M} w_{k,m}
    },
    \label{eq:clean_estimate_U_prelim}
\end{equation}
which yields the model-based Monte Carlo score estimate
\begin{equation}
    \hat S_k^{\mathrm{mb}}
    =
    -
    \frac{U_k}{1-\bar\alpha_k}
    +
    \frac{\sqrt{\bar\alpha_k}}{1-\bar\alpha_k}
    \bar U_k .
    \label{eq:mb_score_U}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Constraint Manifolds}
\label{subsec:prelim_constraint_manifold}

For equality constraints $h(s,u,c)=0$ and inequality constraints $g(s,u,c)\le 0$, we introduce
nonnegative slack variables through
\begin{equation}
    g(s,u,c)+\frac{1}{2}\mu\odot \mu=0.
\end{equation}
For a rolled-out control sequence $U$ and its induced state sequence $s_{0:H}$, define
\begin{equation}
    C(U,\mu;s_0,c)
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
    h(s_h,u_h,c)\\
    g(s_h,u_h,c)+\frac{1}{2}\mu_h\odot\mu_h
    \end{bmatrix}_{h=0}^{H-1}.
    \label{eq:constraint_vector}
\end{equation}
The lower-control safety manifold is
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal M(s_0,c)
    =
    \left\{
    (U,\mu):
    C(U,\mu;s_0,c)=0,\;
    K_h=\exp(S_h),\;
    h=0,\ldots,H-1
    \right\}.
    \label{eq:control_manifold}
\end{equation}
For contact-rich systems with multiple contact modes, such as hand-object face selection or
stance-foot selection, the feasible set is a hybrid union
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal M(s_0,c)
    =
    \bigcup_{m\in\mathcal A}
    \mathcal M_m(s_0,c),
    \label{eq:hybrid_union_manifold}
\end{equation}
where $m$ indexes the active contact mode.

Given a metric $G(U)\succ 0$ and the constraint Jacobian
\begin{equation}
    J_C(U)=\nabla_U C(U,\mu;s_0,c),
\end{equation}
the metric-aware tangent projection is
\begin{equation}
    P_{\mathcal M}^{G}(U)
    =
    I
    -
    G^{-1}J_C^\top
    \left(
    J_CG^{-1}J_C^\top
    \right)^\dagger
    J_C .
    \label{eq:metric_tangent_projection}
\end{equation}
This operator projects score directions and stochastic perturbations onto the tangent space of the
active lower-control manifold.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Overview}
\label{subsec:method_overview}

We propose \emph{Manifold Diffusion Annealing Control} (MDAC), a joint model-free and
model-based lower-control framework. The method samples and denoises a lower-control sequence
$U=u_{0:H-1}$, rolls it out through the closed-loop dynamics to obtain $s_{1:H}$, evaluates task and
safety objectives, and updates $U$ through a geometry-aware reverse diffusion process.

The main idea is to define a target distribution over lower-control sequences,
\begin{equation}
    p^\star(U\mid s_0,c)
    \propto
    p_{\psi}(U\mid s_0,c)^{\lambda_\psi}
    \exp
    \left(
    -\frac{1}{\beta}
    J_{\mathrm{cl}}(U;s_0,c)
    \right)
    \mathbf 1_{\mathcal M(s_0,c)}(U),
    \label{eq:joint_target_hard}
\end{equation}
where $p_{\psi}$ is a model-free RL prior and the exponential term is a model-based closed-loop
likelihood evaluated by rollout. The indicator restricts the support to the active contact/safety
manifold. In practice, the hard indicator is replaced by a soft residual during score estimation and
a hard retraction after denoising.

\subsection{Model-Free RL Prior over Control Sequences}
\label{subsec:method_rl_prior}

We first train a model-free lower-control policy
\begin{equation}
    \pi_\psi(u_h\mid o_h,c),
\end{equation}
where $o_h$ is the observation induced by the rolled-out state $s_h$. The policy is trained with a
standard RL objective
\begin{equation}
    \max_\psi
    \mathbb E_{\pi_\psi}
    \left[
    \sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^t r(s_t,u_t,c)
    \right],
    \label{eq:rl_objective}
\end{equation}
where the reward includes task progress, tracking, contact quality, stiffness regularization, and soft
safety penalties. The RL policy is not used as the final controller. Instead, it provides a model-free
prior over promising lower-control sequences:
\begin{equation}
    p_\psi(U\mid s_0,c)
    =
    \prod_{h=0}^{H-1}
    \pi_\psi(u_h\mid \hat o_h,c),
    \qquad
    \hat s_{h+1}=F(\hat s_h,u_h;c).
    \label{eq:rl_prior_horizon}
\end{equation}
This prior guides the generative controller toward useful contact modes, nominal motion directions,
and reasonable stiffness profiles.

At runtime, the RL prior is injected in two ways. First, it warm-starts the initial control sequence:
\begin{equation}
    U^{\mathrm{init}}
    =
    \lambda_{\mathrm{shift}}U^{\mathrm{shift}}
    +
    (1-\lambda_{\mathrm{shift}})U^{\mathrm{rl}},
    \label{eq:rl_warm_start}
\end{equation}
where $U^{\mathrm{shift}}$ is the shifted solution from the previous control step and $U^{\mathrm{rl}}$
is a rollout of $\pi_\psi$. Second, it contributes to the importance weight of each candidate through
$\log p_\psi(\tilde U_{k,m}\mid s_0,c)$.

\subsection{Soft Model-Based Feasibility for Score Estimation}
\label{subsec:method_soft_feasibility}

Directly using the hard indicator $\mathbf 1_{\mathcal M}$ can cause weight collapse, especially in
high-dimensional contact-rich systems. We therefore define an adaptive soft feasibility objective
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \tilde J_k(U;s_0,c)
    =
    J_{\mathrm{cl}}(U;s_0,c)
    +
    \lambda_k^\top r(U;s_0,c)
    +
    \frac{\rho_k}{2}\|r(U;s_0,c)\|^2,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:soft_feasibility_cost}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
    r(U;s_0,c)
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
    h(s_h,u_h,c)\\
    [g(s_h,u_h,c)]_+
    \end{bmatrix}_{h=0}^{H-1}
    \label{eq:constraint_residual}
\end{equation}
is evaluated after rolling out $U$ through Eq.~\eqref{eq:closed_loop_rollout}. The adaptive
parameters $\lambda_k$ and $\rho_k$ are updated across reverse steps so that early denoising
keeps informative near-feasible samples, while late denoising enforces strict feasibility.

The soft target used for score estimation is
\begin{equation}
    \tilde p_k(U\mid s_0,c)
    \propto
    p_\psi(U\mid s_0,c)^{\lambda_{\psi,k}}
    \exp
    \left(
    -\frac{1}{\beta_k}
    \tilde J_k(U;s_0,c)
    \right).
    \label{eq:soft_target_distribution}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Model-Based Monte Carlo Score in Control Space}
\label{subsec:method_mc_score}

At reverse step $k$, given the current noisy control sequence $U_k$, we draw $M$ clean control
candidates
\begin{equation}
    \tilde U_{k,m}
    \sim
    \Omega_k(U_k)
    =
    \mathcal N
    \left(
    \frac{U_k}{\sqrt{\bar\alpha_k}},
    \left(
    \frac{1}{\bar\alpha_k}-1
    \right)\Sigma_k
    \right),
    \qquad m=1,\ldots,M.
    \label{eq:method_clean_proposal}
\end{equation}
Each candidate is rolled out through the closed-loop dynamics:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde s_{h+1}^{m}
    =
    F(\tilde s_h^{m},\tilde u_{k,m,h};c).
    \label{eq:method_candidate_rollout}
\end{equation}
We then compute the log weight
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \log w_{k,m}
    =
    -
    \frac{1}{\beta_k}
    \tilde J_k(\tilde U_{k,m};s_0,c)
    +
    \lambda_{\psi,k}
    \log p_\psi(\tilde U_{k,m}\mid s_0,c).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:importance_weight}
\end{equation}
The normalized weights are
\begin{equation}
    \bar w_{k,m}
    =
    \frac{\exp(\log w_{k,m})}
    {\sum_{j=1}^{M}\exp(\log w_{k,j})}.
\end{equation}
The weighted clean control estimate is
\begin{equation}
    \bar U_k
    =
    \sum_{m=1}^{M}\bar w_{k,m}\tilde U_{k,m}.
    \label{eq:method_clean_mean}
\end{equation}
This gives the model-based control-space score estimate
\begin{equation}
    \hat S_k^{\mathrm{mb}}
    =
    -
    \frac{U_k}{1-\bar\alpha_k}
    +
    \frac{\sqrt{\bar\alpha_k}}{1-\bar\alpha_k}
    \bar U_k.
    \label{eq:method_mb_score}
\end{equation}

If a learned diffusion prior over control sequences is available, with score
$s_\theta(U_k,k,s_0,c)$, we combine it with the model-based score:
\begin{equation}
    \hat S_k
    =
    \omega_k^{\mathrm{mb}}\hat S_k^{\mathrm{mb}}
    +
    \omega_k^{\mathrm{mf}}s_\theta(U_k,k,s_0,c).
    \label{eq:joint_score}
\end{equation}
In the minimal implementation, we set $\omega_k^{\mathrm{mf}}=0$ and inject the model-free
knowledge through RL warm-start and the likelihood term in Eq.~\eqref{eq:importance_weight}.

\subsection{Stiffness- and Constraint-Aware Metric Shaping}
\label{subsec:method_metric}

The raw score direction in Eq.~\eqref{eq:method_mb_score} ignores the geometry of the active
constraints and the positive-definite stiffness space. We define a task-dependent metric
\begin{equation}
    G_k(U)
    =
    I
    +
    \rho_k J_C(U)^\top W_CJ_C(U)
    +
    \kappa_k J_{\mathrm{tr}}(U)^\top W_{\mathrm{tr}}J_{\mathrm{tr}}(U)
    +
    G_K(U),
    \label{eq:method_metric}
\end{equation}
where $J_C$ is the active constraint Jacobian, $J_{\mathrm{tr}}$ measures closed-loop tracking or
contact sensitivity, and $G_K$ is the stiffness geometry term. For the log-Euclidean stiffness
parametrization, a simple choice is
\begin{equation}
    G_K(U)
    =
    \mathrm{blkdiag}(0,\ldots,W_S,\ldots,0),
\end{equation}
where $W_S$ weights the log-stiffness coordinates $S_h$. The geometry-shaped score is
\begin{equation}
    \hat S_{\mathcal M,k}
    =
    P_{\mathcal M}^{G_k}(U_k)
    G_k(U_k)^{-1}
    \hat S_k,
    \label{eq:geometry_shaped_score}
\end{equation}
and the reverse stochasticity is filtered through the same tangent projector:
\begin{equation}
    \epsilon_{\mathcal M,k}
    =
    P_{\mathcal M}^{G_k}(U_k)\epsilon_k,
    \qquad
    \epsilon_k\sim\mathcal N(0,I).
    \label{eq:tangent_noise}
\end{equation}
Thus, both the ascent direction and the exploration noise are constrained by the active
lower-control manifold.

\subsection{Manifold Diffusion Annealing Update}
\label{subsec:method_reverse_update}

Given the shaped score in Eq.~\eqref{eq:geometry_shaped_score}, we use a DDPM-style stochastic
reverse update in control space:
\begin{equation}
    U_{k-1}^{\mathrm{raw}}
    =
    \frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_k}}
    \left(
    U_k
    +
    (1-\bar\alpha_k)\hat S_{\mathcal M,k}
    \right)
    +
    \sigma_k\epsilon_{\mathcal M,k}.
    \label{eq:ddpm_control_update}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, a DDIM or flow-like update can be written through the predicted clean control
sequence
\begin{equation}
    \hat U_{1,k}
    =
    \frac{
    U_k+(1-\bar\alpha_k)\hat S_{\mathcal M,k}
    }{
    \sqrt{\bar\alpha_k}
    },
    \label{eq:predicted_clean_control}
\end{equation}
and the implied noise
\begin{equation}
    \hat\epsilon_k
    =
    \frac{
    U_k-\sqrt{\bar\alpha_k}\hat U_{1,k}
    }{
    \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_k}
    }.
\end{equation}
The generalized DDIM update is
\begin{equation}
    U_{k-1}^{\mathrm{raw}}
    =
    \sqrt{\bar\alpha_{k-1}}\hat U_{1,k}
    +
    \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_{k-1}-\sigma_k^2}\hat\epsilon_k
    +
    \sigma_k\epsilon_{\mathcal M,k}.
    \label{eq:ddim_control_update}
\end{equation}
When $\sigma_k>0$, the update is stochastic and diffusion-like. When $\sigma_k=0$, it becomes a
deterministic flow-like lower-control refinement.

After the raw update, we retract the sequence back to the active manifold:
\begin{equation}
    U_{k-1}
    =
    \operatorname{Retr}_{\mathcal M}
    \left(
    U_{k-1}^{\mathrm{raw}}
    \right).
    \label{eq:method_retraction}
\end{equation}
The retraction is defined as the local metric projection
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \operatorname{Retr}_{\mathcal M}(U^{\mathrm{raw}})
    =
    \arg\min_U
    \quad&
    \frac{1}{2}
    \|U-U^{\mathrm{raw}}\|_{G_k}^2
    \\
    \mathrm{s.t.}\quad&
    C(U,\mu;s_0,c)=0,
    \qquad
    K_h=\exp(S_h).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:retraction_qp}
\end{equation}
In practice, Eq.~\eqref{eq:retraction_qp} is solved approximately by one or two linearized
projection steps:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \Delta U^\star
    =
    \arg\min_{\Delta U}
    \quad&
    \frac{1}{2}\|\Delta U\|_{G_k}^2
    \\
    \mathrm{s.t.}\quad&
    C(U)+J_C(U)\Delta U=0,
    \\
    &
    g(U)+J_g(U)\Delta U\le 0.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:linearized_retraction}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Adaptive Annealing of Noise, Constraints, and Stiffness}
\label{subsec:method_schedule}

The reverse process uses a coupled schedule
\begin{equation}
    \left(
    \sigma_k,\rho_k,\kappa_k,\lambda_{\psi,k}
    \right),
    \label{eq:coupled_schedule}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_k$ controls reverse stochasticity, $\rho_k$ controls constraint hardening,
$\kappa_k$ controls tracking and stiffness hardening, and $\lambda_{\psi,k}$ controls the strength
of the RL prior. We use the qualitative schedule
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_k\downarrow,
    \qquad
    \rho_k\uparrow,
    \qquad
    \kappa_k\uparrow,
    \qquad
    \lambda_{\psi,k}\downarrow.
    \label{eq:schedule_trend}
\end{equation}
Early reverse steps therefore use the RL prior and stochasticity to explore useful contact modes and
motion directions. Later steps emphasize model-based rollout cost, constraint satisfaction, and
manifold retraction.

\subsection{Receding-Horizon Execution}
\label{subsec:method_receding_horizon}

At every real control step, MDAC solves the lower-control sampling problem for the current state,
executes the first control primitive, shifts the remaining sequence, and repeats. The algorithm is
summarized below.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Manifold Diffusion Annealing Control}
\label{alg:mdac}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE \textbf{Input:} current state $s_0$, context $c$, RL prior $\pi_\psi$, horizon $H$, reverse steps $K$, samples $M$
\STATE Roll out $\pi_\psi$ to obtain $U^{\mathrm{rl}}$
\STATE Shift previous solution to obtain $U^{\mathrm{shift}}$
\STATE Initialize $U_K$ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:rl_warm_start} with tangent noise
\FOR{$k=K,\ldots,1$}
    \STATE Sample clean candidates $\tilde U_{k,m}\sim\Omega_k(U_k)$
    \STATE Roll out each candidate through $\tilde s_{h+1}^{m}=F(\tilde s_h^{m},\tilde u_{k,m,h};c)$
    \STATE Compute $\tilde J_k(\tilde U_{k,m};s_0,c)$ and $\log p_\psi(\tilde U_{k,m}\mid s_0,c)$
    \STATE Compute normalized weights $\bar w_{k,m}$ using Eq.~\eqref{eq:importance_weight}
    \STATE Estimate $\bar U_k$ and $\hat S_k^{\mathrm{mb}}$ using Eqs.~\eqref{eq:method_clean_mean}--\eqref{eq:method_mb_score}
    \STATE Form joint score $\hat S_k$ using Eq.~\eqref{eq:joint_score}
    \STATE Compute $G_k$, $P_{\mathcal M}^{G_k}$, $\hat S_{\mathcal M,k}$, and $\epsilon_{\mathcal M,k}$
    \STATE Update $U_{k-1}^{\mathrm{raw}}$ using Eq.~\eqref{eq:ddpm_control_update} or Eq.~\eqref{eq:ddim_control_update}
    \STATE Retract $U_{k-1}=\operatorname{Retr}_{\mathcal M}(U_{k-1}^{\mathrm{raw}})$
\ENDFOR
\STATE Execute $u_0^\star=U_1[0]$ through the lower-control interface
\STATE Store optimized sequence for optional prior distillation
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Research Questions}
\label{subsec:experiment_questions}

We design experiments to answer the following questions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RQ1:} Does sampling lower-control primitives $U$ outperform trajectory-level or torque-only sampling in contact-rich tasks?
    \item \textbf{RQ2:} Does the position--stiffness primitive improve the safety--task tradeoff compared with fixed-stiffness or position-only controllers?
    \item \textbf{RQ3:} Does manifold tangent denoising and retraction reduce contact, force, and balance violations compared with sequential safety filters?
    \item \textbf{RQ4:} Does the joint model-free/model-based formulation improve sample efficiency and generalization compared with model-free-only and model-based-only methods?
    \item \textbf{RQ5:} Can the same formulation handle both manipulation and humanoid loco-manipulation through different definitions of $u_t$, $F$, and $\mathcal M$?
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Experiment I: Robotic Surface-Contact Manipulation}
\label{subsec:exp_arm_surface}

The first experiment studies a 7-DoF manipulator performing contact-rich scanning over curved
surfaces. The environment is parameterized by a NURBS surface
\begin{equation}
    p_s(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb R^3,
    \qquad
    (\xi,\eta)\in[0,1]^2.
\end{equation}
The surface normal is
\begin{equation}
    n_s(\xi,\eta)
    =
    \frac{
    \partial_\xi p_s(\xi,\eta)\times\partial_\eta p_s(\xi,\eta)
    }{
    \|\partial_\xi p_s(\xi,\eta)\times\partial_\eta p_s(\xi,\eta)\|
    }.
\end{equation}
The lower-control primitive is
\begin{equation}
    u_h^{\mathrm{arm}}
    =
    (\Delta\xi_h,\Delta\eta_h,\Delta\psi_h,S_h,F_{n,h}^{d}),
    \qquad
    K_h=\exp(S_h).
\end{equation}
The desired end-effector pose is reconstructed from the surface coordinates:
\begin{equation}
    p_h^{d}
    =
    p_s(\xi_h,\eta_h)+d\,n_s(\xi_h,\eta_h),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    R_h^{d}e_z
    =
    -n_s(\xi_h,\eta_h),
\end{equation}
with yaw $\psi_h$ around the normal direction. The impedance controller maps the primitive to
joint torques:
\begin{equation}
    F_h
    =
    K_h(p_h^d-p_h)
    +
    D_h(\dot p_h^d-\dot p_h)
    +
    F_{n,h}^{d}n_s,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \tau_h
    =
    J(q_h)^\top F_h+\tau_{\mathrm{null}}.
\end{equation}
The main surface-contact constraints are
\begin{equation}
    h_{\mathrm{surf}}(s_h,u_h)
    =
    p_h-p_s(\xi_h,\eta_h)-d\,n_s(\xi_h,\eta_h)=0,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    h_{\mathrm{normal}}(s_h,u_h)
    =
    R_h e_z+n_s(\xi_h,\eta_h)=0,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    F_{\min}\le F_{n,h}\le F_{\max}.
\end{equation}
The task objective is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    J_{\mathrm{arm}}
    =
    &
    w_{\mathrm{path}}
    \sum_{h=0}^{H-1}
    \|(\xi_h,\eta_h)-(\xi_h^\star,\eta_h^\star)\|^2
    +
    w_F
    \sum_{h=0}^{H-1}
    (F_{n,h}-F_{n}^{d})^2
    \\
    &
    +
    w_R
    \sum_{h=0}^{H-1}
    \|R_he_z+n_s(\xi_h,\eta_h)\|^2
    +
    w_K
    \sum_{h=0}^{H-1}
    \|\log K_h-\log K_{\mathrm{ref}}\|^2
    \\
    &
    +
    w_{\Delta K}
    \sum_{h=0}^{H-2}
    \|\log K_{h+1}-\log K_h\|^2 .
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:arm_cost}
\end{equation}

We evaluate four surface families:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{S1:} planar and cylindrical surfaces;
    \item \textbf{S2:} convex ellipsoid-like surfaces;
    \item \textbf{S3:} nonconvex NURBS surfaces with local bumps and valleys;
    \item \textbf{S4:} unseen NURBS surfaces with randomized surface stiffness and contact friction.
\end{itemize}

The metrics are surface tracking error, normal alignment error, force tracking error, force violation
rate, force CVaR95, contact loss rate, path completion, coverage ratio, stiffness smoothness, energy,
and runtime.

\subsection{Experiment II: Humanoid Contact-Manifold Box Pushing}
\label{subsec:exp_humanoid_box}

The second experiment studies humanoid loco-manipulation in a box-pushing task. The humanoid
must move a box to a goal region while maintaining hand-object contact, foot-ground contact,
balance, friction feasibility, and non-tipping constraints.

The object state is
\begin{equation}
    x_b=(p_{b,x},p_{b,y},\theta_b)\in SE(2),
\end{equation}
embedded in $SE(3)$ by the ground-contact manifold
\begin{equation}
    h_{\mathrm{box}}(x_b)
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
    p_{b,z}-z_0\\
    \phi_b\\
    \vartheta_b
    \end{bmatrix}
    =
    0,
    \label{eq:box_ground_manifold}
\end{equation}
where $\phi_b$ and $\vartheta_b$ are box roll and pitch.

The hand contact is parameterized by a box face index $j$ and local face coordinates $(a,b)$:
\begin{equation}
    c_h^{\mathrm{hand}}
    =
    (j_h,a_h,b_h).
\end{equation}
Let $p_{\mathrm{face}}^j(a,b)$ be the local point on the selected box face. The world contact point is
\begin{equation}
    p_c
    =
    T_b p_{\mathrm{face}}^j(a,b).
\end{equation}
The hand-box contact manifold is
\begin{equation}
    h_{\mathrm{hand}}(s_h,u_h)
    =
    p_{\mathrm{hand}}(q_h)
    -
    T_b p_{\mathrm{face}}^{j_h}(a_h,b_h)
    =
    0,
    \label{eq:hand_box_contact}
\end{equation}
with orientation consistency
\begin{equation}
    h_{\mathrm{hand},R}(s_h,u_h)
    =
    R_{\mathrm{hand}}e_z
    +
    R_b n_{j_h}
    =
    0.
    \label{eq:hand_box_orientation}
\end{equation}
For stance feet, we impose
\begin{equation}
    h_{\mathrm{foot}}^i(s_h)
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
    p_{f_i,z}(q_h)\\
    R_{f_i}e_z-e_z\\
    v_{f_i}(q_h,\dot q_h)
    \end{bmatrix}
    =
    0,
    \qquad i\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{stance}}.
    \label{eq:foot_contact_manifold}
\end{equation}
Balance and contact safety are encoded as inequalities:
\begin{equation}
    g_{\mathrm{bal}}(s_h)\le 0,
    \qquad
    g_{\mathrm{fric}}(s_h,u_h)=\|f_{t,h}\|-\mu f_{n,h}\le 0,
    \qquad
    g_{\mathrm{tip}}(x_{b,h},f_h)\le 0.
    \label{eq:humanoid_ineq}
\end{equation}
The complete humanoid contact manifold is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \mathcal M_{\mathrm{hum}}
    =
    \bigcup_{m\in\mathcal A}
    \{
    U:
    &
    h_{\mathrm{box}}=0,\;
    h_{\mathrm{hand}}^m=0,\;
    h_{\mathrm{hand},R}^m=0,\;
    h_{\mathrm{foot}}^m=0,
    \\
    &
    g_{\mathrm{bal}}+\frac{1}{2}\mu_{\mathrm{bal}}^2=0,\;
    g_{\mathrm{fric}}+\frac{1}{2}\mu_{\mathrm{fric}}^2=0,\;
    g_{\mathrm{tip}}+\frac{1}{2}\mu_{\mathrm{tip}}^2=0,\;
    K=\exp(S)
    \}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:humanoid_manifold}
\end{equation}
The lower-control primitive is
\begin{equation}
    u_h^{\mathrm{hum}}
    =
    (
    v_h^{\mathrm{base}},
    p_h^{\mathrm{foot}},
    j_h,a_h,b_h,
    S_h^{\mathrm{hand}},
    S_h^{\mathrm{body}},
    F_{n,h}^{d}
    ).
\end{equation}
The humanoid cost is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    J_{\mathrm{hum}}
    =
    &
    w_b\|x_{b,H}-x_b^\star\|^2
    +
    w_{\mathrm{prog}}\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}
    \ell_{\mathrm{prog}}(x_{b,h},x_b^\star)
    +
    w_{\mathrm{bal}}\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}
    [g_{\mathrm{bal}}(s_h)]_+^2
    \\
    &
    +
    w_{\mathrm{fric}}\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}
    [g_{\mathrm{fric}}(s_h,u_h)]_+^2
    +
    w_{\mathrm{tip}}\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}
    [g_{\mathrm{tip}}(x_{b,h},f_h)]_+^2
    \\
    &
    +
    w_{\mathrm{hand}}\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}
    \|h_{\mathrm{hand}}(s_h,u_h)\|^2
    +
    w_{\mathrm{foot}}\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}
    \|h_{\mathrm{foot}}(s_h,u_h)\|^2
    \\
    &
    +
    w_K\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}
    \|\log K_h-\log K_{\mathrm{ref}}\|^2
    +
    w_{\Delta K}\sum_{h=0}^{H-2}
    \|\log K_{h+1}-\log K_h\|^2.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:humanoid_cost}
\end{equation}

We evaluate four task levels:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{H1:} double-support box pushing with fixed feet;
    \item \textbf{H2:} heavy-box pushing with randomized mass and friction;
    \item \textbf{H3:} walk-and-push with stance-foot switching;
    \item \textbf{H4:} box unjamming with local contact-face selection.
\end{itemize}

The metrics are box-goal success rate, fall rate, box pose error, hand contact loss, hand tangential
slip, friction-cone violation, tipping margin, foot slip, balance margin, force CVaR95, stiffness
smoothness, energy, and runtime.

\subsection{Implementation Details}
\label{subsec:exp_implementation}

For each control step, all sampling-based methods use the same horizon $H$, reverse or optimization
iterations $K$, and sample budget $M$. The RL prior is trained using PPO for humanoid tasks and
SAC for manipulator tasks. In the minimal implementation, the RL prior is used for warm-starting
and likelihood weighting, without training a diffusion prior. In the full implementation, optimized
control sequences are stored in an elite buffer and used to train a conditional diffusion prior
$s_\theta(U_k,k,s_0,c)$.

All methods execute only the first control primitive $u_0^\star$ and then replan in a receding-horizon
fashion. The sampled object is always $U$; the state sequence $s_{1:H}$ is only obtained by the
closed-loop rollout in Eq.~\eqref{eq:closed_loop_rollout}.

# MGA: Manifold Generative Annealing for Contact Impedance Control

> A realization-aware joint model-free / model-based framework for rigid and compliant contact control.

---

## 1. Overview

MGA is a receding-horizon contact impedance control framework that combines:

1. **A model-free reinforcement learning prior** for proposing behaviorally meaningful position–stiffness control sequences.
2. **A model-based generative annealing optimizer** for short-horizon physical verification and local refinement.
3. **A realization-aware manifold mechanism** that lifts clean geometry through
   a frozen short-horizon closed-loop response map.
4. **An incumbent fallback and trust-region mechanism** that prevents the
   model-based stage from unnecessarily degrading the model-free policy. The
   fallback is called safe only after its realized risk has been validated; an
   under-trained RL incumbent is not safe by construction.

The central principles are:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Model-free learning decides where to search; model-based rollout decides what currently works.}
}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\text{Geometry constrains predicted realized behavior, not merely clean command coordinates.}
}
\]

MGA is intended to provide a unified architecture across rigid and compliant
contact without assuming that one fixed clean-command manifold is reliable in
every contact regime. Geometry is retained as a structured search prior, while
the local response lift is regularized, clipped, and trust scaled according to
the validated safety--performance frontier.

---

## 2. Motivation

For rigid contact, clean geometry often predicts the realized physical outcome
well over short horizons:

\[
\mathcal M_{\text{clean}}
\approx
\mathcal M_{\text{real}}.
\]

In this regime, tangent projection and retraction can accelerate optimization
and improve feasibility. This is not an unconditional rigid-versus-soft
distinction: long-horizon tracking lag, actuator limits, friction, or model
mismatch can also make a rigid clean-command manifold disagree with the realized
trajectory.

For soft contact, the same geometric command may lead to different realized outcomes because force and deformation depend on material properties, damping, contact history, friction, and the simulator or real-world contact process:

\[
\mathcal M_{\text{clean}}
\neq
\mathcal M_{\text{real}}.
\]

A wrong manifold is therefore not a neutral prior. It becomes an active optimization bias that can pull a good reinforcement-learning or reward-based solution toward a physically suboptimal command.

MGA therefore treats geometry according to measured or predicted
command-to-realization consistency:

- **Controllable directions:** clean geometry corrected by a regularized local
  response map.
- **Weak or uncertain response:** conservative trust, with the outcome
  determined by realized rollout and later RL-incumbent acceptance.
- **Uncertain refinement:** acceptance only when the refined sequence is
  predicted to improve over the incumbent proposal.

The current CPU evidence motivates this formulation: unconditional retraction
can force command progress while increasing realized path error; removing all
geometry can reduce local error by simply failing to progress. MGA must solve
both failure modes simultaneously rather than interpolate between them.

## 3. Problem Formulation

At each control step, MGA optimizes a sequence of lower-control primitives

\[
U = u_{0:H-1}.
\]

The state trajectory is not sampled directly. It is generated by closed-loop rollout:

\[
s_{h+1}=F(s_h,u_h;c),
\]

where \(c\) contains environment and task context.

The dynamics can include a fixed low-level controller:

\[
a_h=\pi_{\mathrm{low}}(s_h,u_h;c),
\]

\[
F(s_h,u_h;c)=f(s_h,a_h).
\]

The target control distribution is

\[
p^\star(U\mid s,c)
\propto
p_\psi(U\mid s,c)^{\lambda_\psi}
\exp\left(-\frac{J_{\mathrm{real}}(U;s,c)}{\beta}\right),
\]

optionally combined with reliable geometric constraints.

Here:

- \(p_\psi\) is the model-free RL prior.
- \(J_{\mathrm{real}}\) is the short-horizon task and risk cost evaluated through rollout.
- \(\beta\) is the annealing temperature.
- \(\lambda_\psi\) controls the influence of the RL prior.

For the CPU geometry-validation stages, \(p_\psi\) is intentionally absent
(``prior=None``). These stages isolate geometry, reliability, and safety
mechanisms. The RL prior, trust region, and fallback are introduced only in the
GPU phase after the geometry mechanism passes the CPU acceptance tests.

## 4. Control Primitive

### 4.1 Arm Contact Impedance Primitive

For surface-contact scanning:

\[
u_t^{\mathrm{arm}}
=
(\Delta \xi_t,\Delta \eta_t,\Delta\psi_t,S_t,F_{n,t}^{d})
\in \mathbb R^{10}.
\]

The translational stiffness is represented on the SPD manifold:

\[
K_t=\exp(S_t),
\qquad
S_t=S_t^\top\in\mathbb S^3.
\]

The primitive controls:

- Tangential motion on the surface.
- In-plane probe orientation.
- Desired normal force.
- Full translational Cartesian stiffness.

### 4.2 Humanoid Rear-Contact Unjamming Primitive

For rear-contact box unjamming:

\[
u_t^{\mathrm{H4}}
=
(a_t,b_t,\alpha_t,S_t^{\mathrm{hand}},F_{n,t}^{d})
\in \mathbb R^{10}.
\]

The primitive controls:

- Rear-surface contact coordinate \((a,b)\).
- Small pushing angle \(\alpha\).
- Hand stiffness.
- Desired normal force.

The humanoid remains behind the box. It releases the jam by selecting an off-center rear contact point, changing the pushing direction, and regulating force and stiffness.

## 5. Low-Level Impedance Control

### 5.1 Arm

A Cartesian impedance controller executes each primitive:

\[
F_t
=
K_t(p_t^d-p_t)
+
D_t(\dot p_t^d-\dot p_t)
+
F_{n,t}^{d}n_t,
\]

\[
\tau_t=J(q_t)^\top F_t+\tau_{\mathrm{null}}.
\]

Surface geometry provides coordinates and approximate normal direction:

\[
p_t^d=S(\xi_t,\eta_t)+d_tn(\xi_t,\eta_t).
\]

For compliant contact, the normal penetration \(d_t\) is not treated as a universally correct hard geometric target.

### 5.2 Humanoid

The initial implementation uses:

\[
\pi_{\mathrm{low}}
=
\text{fixed double-support controller}
+
\text{hand Cartesian impedance}
+
\text{simple balance stabilizer}.
\]

A full whole-body controller can be added later, but is not required for the first H4 benchmark.

## 6. Model-Free RL Prior

The RL policy provides a behavior prior:

\[
u_t^{\mathrm{RL}}\sim\pi_\psi(u_t\mid o_t,c).
\]

A horizon-level proposal is obtained by rolling out the policy:

\[
U^{\mathrm{RL}}
=
(u_0^{\mathrm{RL}},\ldots,u_{H-1}^{\mathrm{RL}}).
\]

The RL prior is responsible for:

- Learning contact behavior from data.
- Providing reasonable stiffness and force choices.
- Encoding longer-term experience.
- Reducing the variance of model-based search.
- Producing the incumbent fallback when model-based refinement is uncertain;
  its safety remains an empirical training/evaluation requirement.

The same trained policy is used as:

1. The standalone RL baseline.
2. The proposal prior for MGA.

No additional RL interaction is required during test-time refinement.

## 7. Model-Based Generative Annealing

### 7.1 Candidate Sampling

Candidates are sampled near the RL proposal:

\[
\tilde U_{k,m}=U_k+\epsilon_{k,m},
\]

or from a mixture proposal:

\[
q_k(U)
=
(1-\lambda_q)q_{\mathrm{diff}}(U\mid U_k)
+
\lambda_q q_\psi(U\mid s,c).
\]

For hybrid or multimodal tasks, candidates may be stratified by contact mode or geometric region.

### 7.2 Closed-Loop Rollout

Each candidate is evaluated using

\[
s_{h+1}^{m}=F(s_h^{m},\tilde u_h^{m};c).
\]

For rigid contact, \(F\) may be a rigid-body simulator or analytical model.

For compliant contact, \(F\) may be:

- A deformable-contact simulator.
- A learned contact-response model.
- A nominal model plus a learned residual.
- A real rollout in a controlled experimental setting.

### 7.3 Realized Objective

The candidate cost is based on realized outcomes:

\[
J_{\mathrm{real}}
=
J_{\mathrm{task}}
+
J_{\mathrm{force}}
+
J_{\mathrm{deformation}}
+
J_{\mathrm{contact}}
+
J_{\mathrm{smoothness}}
+
J_{\mathrm{risk}}.
\]

For soft surface scanning, a typical objective is

\[
J_{\mathrm{arm}}
=
J_{\mathrm{coverage}}
+
w_FJ_F
+
w_\delta J_\delta
+
w_cJ_{\mathrm{contact-loss}}
+
w_KJ_{\Delta K}.
\]

Coverage is a realized quantity. Command-coordinate progress is logged
separately and cannot substitute for coverage:

\[
J_{\mathrm{coverage}}
=
-\operatorname{ArcLength}
\left(
\Pi_{\mathcal S}\{p^{\mathrm{real}}_{ee,h}\}_{h=0}^{H}
\right),
\]

subject to an on-path/contact mask. A controller that remains near the initial
point may have a small local tracking error but must receive poor coverage.

The current arm environment also exposes an optional, default-off rollout
surrogate

\[
J_{\mathrm{realized-path}}
=
\sum_h
\frac{\left\|
(I-n_hn_h^\top)
\left(p^{\mathrm{real}}_{ee,h}-p^\star_h\right)
\right\|_2^2}
{\epsilon_{\mathrm{path}}^2}.
\]

Projecting out the normal component prevents compliant indentation from being
counted as tangential path error. This surrogate is not itself a coverage
metric: it is accepted only if held-out realized path completion improves. The
CPU weight screen described in Section 21 did not pass that test, so the
surrogate remains off in the canonical Phase-3 sweep.

For box unjamming:

\[
J_{\mathrm{H4}}
=
J_{\mathrm{progress}}
+
J_{\mathrm{yaw}}
+
J_{\mathrm{clearance}}
+
J_{\mathrm{wall}}
+
J_{\mathrm{force}}
+
J_{\mathrm{slip}}
+
J_{\mathrm{balance}}.
\]

### 7.4 Candidate Weighting

\[
\log w_{k,m}
=
-\frac{1}{\beta_k}J_{\mathrm{real}}(\tilde U_{k,m})
+
\lambda_{\psi,k}\log p_\psi(\tilde U_{k,m}\mid s,c).
\]

Normalized weights are

\[
\bar w_{k,m}=\frac{w_{k,m}}{\sum_jw_{k,j}}.
\]

The weighted clean estimate is

\[
\bar U_k=\sum_m\bar w_{k,m}\tilde U_{k,m}.
\]

### 7.5 Model-Based Score

The MBD-style clean posterior estimate gives

\[
\hat S_k^{\mathrm{mb}}
=
-\frac{U_k}{1-\bar\alpha_k}
+
\frac{\sqrt{\bar\alpha_k}}{1-\bar\alpha_k}\bar U_k.
\]

The update can be implemented with a DDPM-style or deterministic flow-like annealing rule.

## 8. Realization-Aware Manifold Guidance

### 8.1 Reliable Geometric Constraints

Geometry remains useful for constraints whose physical meaning is reliable:

- Joint limits.
- Collision avoidance.
- Workspace limits.
- Surface coordinates.
- Tangential motion structure.
- Approximate normal alignment.
- SPD stiffness validity.
- Known rigid contact constraints.

Let

\[
C_{\mathrm{geo}}(U)=0
\]

denote the reliable geometric constraints.

### 8.2 Horizon-Aware Realized Geometry

The clean command sequence is incremental. Its horizon state must therefore be
constructed cumulatively:

\[
\hat \xi_{h+1}
=
\hat \xi_h+\Delta\xi_h,
\qquad
\hat\eta_{h+1}
=
\hat\eta_h+\Delta\eta_h.
\]

The reference is explicitly time indexed:

\[
C^{\mathrm{path}}_h
=
\begin{bmatrix}
\hat\xi_h-\xi_{\mathrm{ref}}(t+h)\\
\hat\eta_h-\eta_{\mathrm{ref}}(t+h)
\end{bmatrix}.
\]

When the rollout model exposes realized end-effector motion, the preferred
consistency residual is

\[
C^{\mathrm{real}}_h
=
p^{\mathrm{rollout}}_{ee,h}
-
S\!\left(\xi_{\mathrm{ref}}(t+h),\eta_{\mathrm{ref}}(t+h)\right).
\]

A residual that applies the same current target independently to every control
node is an ablation, not the final formulation.

### 8.3 Partial Manifold for Soft Contact

For compliant surfaces, the manifold should not hard-code an uncertain force–penetration relationship.

A practical split is

\[
C(U)
=
\begin{bmatrix}
C_{\mathrm{reliable}}(U)\\
C_{\mathrm{uncertain}}(U)
\end{bmatrix}.
\]

Use strong projection only on \(C_{\mathrm{reliable}}\). Force, deformation,
and stiffness are primarily optimized through realized rollout. Removing the
force block does not remove the need for force regulation; it changes force
from a hard clean-command equality to a realized objective and safety term.

### 8.4 Frozen Short-Horizon Controllability Lift

The CPU diagnosis showed that a clean coordinate residual is insufficient even
when it is cumulative and time indexed: the impedance executor may not realize
the requested tangent displacement. The core geometry therefore uses a local
closed-loop response map, estimated around the incumbent dense plan:

\[
B_t
=
\frac{\partial z^{\mathrm{real}}_{t+L}}
{\partial u^{\mathrm{tan}}_t},
\qquad
z=(\xi,\eta).
\]

The current implementation estimates the two task-owned tangent columns by
central differences through \(L=5\) true MJX steps. The chart Jacobian maps the
terminal Cartesian displacement back to \((\xi,\eta)\). The resulting \(B_t\)
is stop-gradient: it changes the residual target but is never differentiated
through the contact rollout.

For current realized coordinate error \(e_t\), use a Tikhonov-regularized
inverse response

\[
\Delta u_t
=
-B_t^\top
\left(B_tB_t^\top+\lambda_B I\right)^{-1}e_t.
\]

After clipping, only a trust-scaled fraction is applied:

\[
\Delta z_h^{\mathrm{target}}
=
\alpha_{\mathrm{resp}}
\min\left(\frac{h+1}{L},1\right)
L\,s_{\mathrm{coord}}\,\Delta u_t.
\]

The clean cumulative path/force geometry remains active. The response lift only
shifts the short-horizon path target; it does not directly modify orientation,
stiffness, or force channels. This preserves the existing solver/environment
separation and avoids a pseudoinverse over the candidate-sample axis.

The trust factor is medium dependent in the current CPU evidence:

- rigid cylinder: \(\alpha_{\mathrm{resp}}=0.5\);
- known soft cylinder: \(\alpha_{\mathrm{resp}}=0.25\);
- held-out soft geometry/material: \(\alpha_{\mathrm{resp}}=0.125\).

These are validated Pareto operating points, not a claim that the medium label
should be known at deployment. Learning or estimating this trust is a Phase-4
RL/confidence task.

### 8.5 Falsified Multiplicative Gate and Revised Reliability Route

Current-error gates, component-wise geometry gates, and gates based on
condition/alignment of \(B_t\) were tested as causal interventions. Although
their logged values sometimes correlated with next-step error, enabling them
did not produce a do-no-harm improvement. On the calibrated rigid two-seed
screen, the best tested \(B_t\)-gate increased contact loss and force-tail cost
relative to the ungated response lift.

Therefore the multiplicative gate is **not part of the validated CPU core**.
It remains only as a falsified ablation. In particular, a bounded or correlated
gate is not accepted as a reliability estimator unless turning it on improves
held-out realized outcomes.

The revised reliability route is:

1. regularize and clip the local response inverse;
2. select a conservative response trust from the measured Pareto frontier;
3. retain the clean recovery geometry instead of disabling it when contact is
   poor;
4. in Phase 4, use RL as a horizon proposal and trust-region center, but compare
   the refined sequence with the shifted previously deployed sequence; reject
   to that receding incumbent rather than to an unverified raw RL sequence.

### 8.6 Safety–Performance Pareto Objective

No single risk weight establishes superiority. For parameter vector
\(\lambda=(w_F,w_\delta,w_c,\alpha_{\mathrm{resp}})\), report the nondominated
set over

\[
\left(
1-\mathrm{coverage},
e_{\mathrm{path}},
e_F,
\operatorname{CVaR}_{0.95}(\delta),
\mathrm{contact\ loss}
\right).
\]

A method is accepted only if it improves the safety–performance frontier rather
than reducing deformation by stopping or leaving the task.

## 9. Model-Based Acceptance and Receding-Incumbent Fallback

The model-based optimizer must not overwrite the deployed plan unconditionally,
and a rejected refinement must not re-enter an unverified raw RL sequence.

### 9.1 RL Proposal as a Candidate

Always include

\[
U^0=U^{\mathrm{RL}}
\]

in the candidate set.

### 9.2 Trust Region

Restrict refinement to the local neighborhood of the prior:

\[
\|U-U^{\mathrm{RL}}\|_{\Sigma_\psi^{-1}}^2\le\epsilon.
\]

### 9.3 Receding Incumbent

After executing the first action of the accepted sequence at time \(t-1\), form

\[
U_t^{\mathrm{I}}
=
\operatorname{HoldTail}\!\left(
\operatorname{Shift}(U_{t-1}^{\star})
\right).
\]

The terminal hold repeats the final previously planned control when extending
the horizon. This is load bearing when several replans are rejected: zero-tail
padding would erase a safe incumbent one step at a time and confound reliability
with an artificial stop. At the cold start no deployed incumbent exists, so the
first model-based refinement establishes \(U_0^{\mathrm{I}}\).

### 9.4 Predicted Improvement and Risk Gate

Define

\[
\Delta J
=
J_{\mathrm{MB}}(U^{\mathrm{refined}})
-
J_{\mathrm{MB}}(U^{\mathrm{I}}),
\]

where larger \(J_{\mathrm{MB}}\) is better. Accept refinement only when

\[
\Delta J>\epsilon_J,
\qquad
R(U^{\mathrm{refined}})
\preceq
R(U^{\mathrm{I}})+\tau,
\]

and the refined horizon predicts no force-limit violation. The realized-risk
vector contains force violation, contact loss, deformation, and force error.

### 9.5 Model Confidence

Let \(\sigma_F\) be predictive uncertainty or an OOD score. The model-based gain is

\[
g_{\mathrm{MB}}=g(\Delta J,\sigma_F,\mathrm{OOD})\in[0,1].
\]

The confidence gate selects between the proposed refinement and the receding
incumbent:

\[
U^\star
=
U^{\mathrm{I}}
+
g_{\mathrm{MB}}(U^{\mathrm{refined}}-U^{\mathrm{I}}).
\]

If the model is uncertain or predicts no improvement:

\[
g_{\mathrm{MB}}=0
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
U^\star=U^{\mathrm{I}}.
\]

This is the main **refinement** do-no-harm mechanism: it prevents a predicted
model-based degradation relative to the deployed receding incumbent. The RL
sequence remains a proposal, candidate, and trust center, but is not the safety
fallback. This remains an empirical bounded-horizon safeguard rather than an
absolute certificate under model mismatch.

## 10. Rigid and Soft Contact Under One Framework

| Component | Rigid Contact | Compliant Contact |
|---|---|---|
| RL prior | Useful | Central |
| Model-based rollout | Rigid dynamics | Learned or realized contact response |
| Geometry reliability | Often high, but horizon-dependent | Partial and state-dependent |
| Tangent geometry | Strong | Still useful |
| Normal penetration geometry | Often reliable | Usually uncertain |
| Projection/retraction | Clean cumulative geometry + response lift | Conservative trust-scaled response lift |
| Force and stiffness | Rollout + geometry | RL + realized rollout |
| Fallback | Shifted deployed incumbent | Shifted deployed incumbent |
| Main model-based role | Optimization and feasibility | Risk checking and OOD adaptation |

The framework is unified through:

- The same primitive representation.
- The same RL-prior interface.
- The same candidate rollout and weighting pipeline.
- The same generative annealing optimizer.
- Contact-regime-dependent geometry and model confidence.

\mathcal D_{\mathrm{sim}}
=
\{o_t,u_t,o_{t+1},F_{n,t+1},\delta_{t+1},c_{\mathrm{loss}},r_t\}.

Use real data for:

- Local force/deformation calibration.

\[
\hat F^{\mathrm{real}}
=
\hat F^{\mathrm{sim}}
+
\Delta_\phi(o,u).
\]

The first real-world version should use safe scripted probing and scanning rather than unrestricted real-world RL exploration.

## 15. Core Evaluation Questions

1. Does the RL prior reduce search variance and improve sample efficiency?
2. Does model-based refinement reduce force, deformation, and contact tail risk?
3. Does realization-aware geometry preserve rigid-contact gains without harming soft contact?
4. Does receding-incumbent fallback prevent negative model-based correction
   without erasing task progress?
5. Does MGA generalize better than standalone RL to unseen compliance, friction, geometry, and jam configurations?
6. Does strong geometry help only when clean constraints correlate with realized outcomes?
7. Can MGA improve the success–violation Pareto frontier rather than only raw reward?

## 16. Algorithm Sketch

```text
Input:
    current observation o_t
    current state s_t
    RL policy π_ψ
    rollout model F
    horizon H
    sample count M

1. Roll out π_ψ to obtain U_RL.
2. Add U_RL to the candidate set.
3. Initialize the annealing trajectory near U_RL.
4. For each annealing step k:
       a. Sample candidate sequences around U_k and U_RL.
       b. Roll out each candidate through F.
       c. Evaluate task, risk, and contact costs.
       d. Compute RL-prior likelihood.
       e. Compute candidate weights.
       f. Estimate the model-based clean posterior / score.
       g. Build cumulative, time-indexed horizon geometry.
       h. Estimate and freeze the short-horizon tangent response map B_t.
       i. Apply the regularized, clipped, trust-scaled response lift.
       j. Perform the annealing update.
5. Form U_I by shifting the previously deployed sequence and holding its tail.
6. Compare refined U with U_I using predicted improvement and realized risk.
7. Apply trust-region acceptance:
       U* = U_I + g_MB (U_refined - U_I)
8. Execute the first primitive u*_0.
9. Recede the horizon and repeat.
```

## 17. Main Claims

MGA should not claim that one fixed contact manifold universally solves both rigid and deformable contact.

The intended claims are staged. Claims 1–2 require the CPU geometry program;
claims 3–4 require the later GPU RL-prior program.

1. **Unified architecture:**  
   A common RL-prior and model-based generative annealing pipeline can be used across rigid and compliant contact.

2. **Realization-aware geometry:**  
   Clean cumulative geometry becomes useful when its target is lifted through a
   measured short-horizon command-to-realization map. The inverse response must
   be regularized and trust limited under contact mismatch.

3. **Joint model-free/model-based control:**  
   Model-free RL provides behavioral competence, while model-based rollout provides local physical verification, risk reduction, and test-time adaptation.

4. **Do-no-harm refinement:**  
   Receding-incumbent fallback, RL-centered trust regions, confidence gating,
   and predicted-improvement checks prevent unreliable model-based updates from
   unnecessarily degrading the deployed closed-loop plan.

5. **Contact impedance generalization:**  
   Position, force, and SPD stiffness are optimized jointly through a common lower-control primitive.

## 18. Key Insight

\[
\boxed{\text{Reliable contact: clean geometry is a useful approximation to realized physics.}}
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Use geometry through a measured local response, and limit its trust when that response is uncertain.}
}
\]

## 20. Staged Validation Roadmap

### Phase 1 — CPU: Geometry Mechanism

Compare, at an identical sampling budget:

1. no geometry / DIAL;
2. cumulative time-indexed clean geometry;
3. frozen short-horizon controllability lift;
4. lift-gain/trust ablations.

Run rigid cylinder, uniform soft, spatially varying compliance, and unseen
low-stiffness NURBS. Acceptance requires realized progress on both seeds,
bounded tangent error, zero force-limit violations, and a nondominated
safety--performance point.

### Phase 2 — CPU: Gate Reliability

Treat every proposed reliability mechanism as an intervention. Correlation and
bin ordering are diagnostics only. A gate is rejected if enabling it worsens
held-out contact/force outcomes relative to the ungated response lift. The
tested current-error, component, and \(B_t\)-condition gates are rejected.

### Phase 3 — CPU: Safety–Performance Pareto

Sweep deformation/force/contact weights and response trust. Report the
nondominated frontier using realized coverage, path error, force error,
deformation CVaR, and contact loss. Select configurations from the frontier,
not from one scalar reward.

### Phase 4 — GPU: RL Prior and Do-No-Harm Refinement

Only after Phases 1–3 pass:

- train and evaluate the shared RL prior;
- insert the RL proposal as a candidate and trust-region center;
- add the prior trust region;
- test predicted-improvement acceptance and receding-incumbent fallback;
- compare standalone RL, model-based-only, and full MGA.

\[
U_t^{\mathrm{I}}
=
\operatorname{HoldTail}\!\left(
\operatorname{Shift}(U_{t-1}^{\star})
\right),
\qquad
U_t^\star
=
\begin{cases}
U_t^{\mathrm{refined}}, & \text{if improvement and risk tests pass},\\
U_t^{\mathrm{I}}, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

Raw RL remains in the candidate set and defines the refinement trust region,

### 21.11 Learned reliability and Soft/OOD safe operating point (2026-08-02)

The remaining CPU work used the existing canonical YAML and result tree; no
per-test YAML variants were introduced. The learned reliability model is a
small standardized multi-output ridge predictor with one-sided split-conformal
upper residuals. Its inputs are twelve pre-action, observable quantities:
path/normal error, signed force error, deformation, contact loss, phase, the
three coordinate actions, raw stiffness-action RMS, commanded-force error, and
action-change RMS. It predicts the existing four-component risk vector

\[
(R_{\rm force},R_{\rm contact},R_{\rm deform},R_{\rm force\text{-}mae}).
\]

Training uses seed-10 transitions; seed-11 transitions are reserved for
conformal calibration. Both accepted full-MGA trajectories and actually
executed no-acceptance refinements are included, giving 594 training
transitions. The calibration set has 792 transitions: 594 disjoint seen-domain
transitions plus 198 deployment-family calibration transitions from unseen
surface seeds 100 and 101. Seeds 102--104 are not used to fit the ridge model or
its residual bound. State and action support scores are calibrated separately;
out-of-support proposals abstain to the receding incumbent.

Two falsifications determine the claim boundary:

1. Seen-only q95 calibration did not transfer to unseen contact: deformation
   and force-MAE coverage fell to 66.2% and 7.1%. A feature-distance abstention
   rule also failed to recover coverage because latent compliance can shift the
   transition target while current observables remain in range. Representative
   deployment-family residual calibration is therefore required; feature
   distance alone is not an OOD certificate.
2. The first online support score was approximately 6.8 because offline data
   used raw stiffness actions while the runtime hook used physical log
   stiffness. After enforcing one feature contract, the score fell to about
   0.59. This contract check is part of the regression gate.

With refinement-distribution data, the frozen checkpoint obtains 95.3%
deformation/force-MAE coverage on its disjoint 792-transition calibration set.
On the independent safe seed-104 trajectory it obtains 96.0% deformation
coverage, 99.0% force-MAE/contact coverage, and 99.0% in-support rate. The
end-to-end learned gate keeps the analytical predicted-improvement test,
requires learned non-degradation within `[0, 0.01, 0.05, 0.05]`, rejects a
learned deformation upper bound above 0.5, and retains the receding incumbent
on rejection. It is active but does not yet improve nominal acceptance or
performance: final acceptance remains 2%. Its present value is calibrated
additional veto/abstention, not a superiority claim.

raw policy, the normal gravity load was about 32.9 N while the commanded force
was about 18.0 N; the force integral spent 98% of the episode saturated at

changed force MAE by only 0.14 N and reduced coverage, so integral capacity was
not the repair. The validated inner-loop point uses partial normal gravity
compensation `grav_comp=0.45` and slower force integration `ki_force=0.25`.

The result is not metric-wise dominance on rigid or known-soft contact. DIAL
traverses the reference aggressively and reaches maximum path progress 1.0,
but its tracking error, force error, deformation tail, command-limit
violations, and control variation are all worse. Full MDAC deliberately pays
runtime and, on the first two suites, maximum-progress cost for a safer and
more accurate operating point. On soft-unseen, however, both methods reach
maximum progress 1.0 and full MDAC also improves coverage while sharply
reducing every reported tracking/contact/force/deformation risk. The main
method claim should therefore be stated as safety--accuracy Pareto superiority
and strong Soft/OOD robustness at fixed sample budget, not universal dominance
over DIAL on raw progress or wall-clock time.
